## 0. Statements (what is proven, what is screened)

(Y-even, Y-odd or mixed).  Proof: (i) `d ≤ 4`: `ker([A]·: R_d → R_{d+6}) = 0` in both parities and `J_d = 0`

forms in 6 unknowns (`relax_d5_mixed.ms`), so `c = 0`.  (Hand proof: `∂_c² F ≡ 0` makes `[c]` a point of
multiplicity ≥ 5 on `X`.)  (iii) `d = 6`: every admissible `D` is `w(F)` with linear `w`; the three relaxation systems

## 1. Mathematics used

### 1.1 Certificate, E1, E2 (q = 1)
`E := A D² − D·N(F) + F·(D div N − N(D)) = F² Q`, `deg N_i = d+1`, `deg Q = 2d−6`.
Reducing mod F (D coprime to the irreducible F): `N(F) = A D + F H`.  Since `F_0..F_5` is a regular sequence,
every field killing F is Koszul, so `N = u + K + (H/6) E` with `u(F) = A D`, `K = Σ_{i<j} c_ij (F_i ∂_j − F_j ∂_i)`,
`E` the Euler field, `deg c_ij = d − 4`.  Substituting (the H-terms cancel):

    E = F · ( Ψ(D;u) + D div K − K(D) ),      Ψ(D;u) := D div u − u(D).

**E1** `A D ∈ J` (existence of u).  **E2'** `Ψ(D;u) + D div K − K(D) = F Q` solvable in `(c_ij, Q)`
("reduced fixed-D system", equivalent to the full one and ~3× smaller; `w2lib.ReducedE2`).

### 1.2 Lemma (E2 modulo J, "Hessian relaxation")
For Koszul K, `D div K − K(D) = Σ_ij [ D (∂_i c_ij) F_j + D c_ij F_ij − c_ij F_j D_i ] ∈ J` (antisymmetry of `c`,
symmetry of the Hessian).  Hence a certificate forces **`Ψ(D;u) ∈ J`**.  `Ψ mod J` does not depend on the choice
of `u` (two choices differ by a Koszul field) and is invariant under `D ↦ D + gF`.
* `D = w(F)` (Jacobian type): `u = A w`, `Ψ ≡ −A · wᵀ Hess(F) w  (mod J)`, i.e. `[wᵀ Hess(F) w] ∈ Ann_R([A])_{2d−6}`.
* `D = λ D8 + w(F)`, `A D8 = u8(F)`: `Ψ ≡ λ²(D8 div u8 − u8(D8)) + λ(D8 div(Aw) − H(u8,w) − A w(D8)) − A H(w,w)`.
In degrees where `[A]·` is injective on `R_d` (d ≤ 7 both parities, d = 8 odd) every E1-admissible D is Jacobian type,
and the relaxation is a homogeneous quadratic system in the coefficients of w (Euler multiples `gE`, which give
`D = 6gF`, are removed by a linear complement: coefficients of X0·m in w_{X0} set to 0).  Mixed Y-parity D is
treated as a separate sector (w = w⁺ + w⁻; the odd part of Ψ is `−A(H(w⁺,w⁺)+H(w⁻,w⁻))`, the even part `−2A H(w⁺,w⁻)`).

### 1.3 Rigor conventions
* Kernel dimension mod p ≥ rational kernel dimension (modular rank is a lower bound): **zero kernel mod p is a proof.**
* Groebner: if the GF(p)-ideal generated by the reduced equations is m-primary (V = {0}), the rational ideal is
  m-primary too: `dim (S/I_p)_k ≥ dim (S/I_Q)_k` for every k because the mod-p equations are the reduction of the
  rational equation lattice.  This needs the mod-p normal forms to be reductions of the rational ones, which holds
  when `dim J_n mod p` equals the Koszul value in the relevant degree n (recorded as `good_reduction` in every receipt).
* Inconsistency of an inhomogeneous linear system mod p proves inconsistency over Q only together with
  `rank_p(M) = rank_Q(M)`, certified by lifting a basis of the mod-p nullspace of M to exact rational vectors
  (`w2_d8.py`, exp `d8_rankcert`).  Without that certificate a modular inconsistency is a screen (two primes = strong screen).
* Sum principle (q = 1, prescribed-support theorem, Chapter 1 of the book): `served(D)` depends only on `X ∩ V(D)`,
  `served(D1 D2) = served(D1) + served(D2)`, and `served(D)/J_6 ⊆ Ann_6(D) := {A' ∈ R_6^- : A' D ∈ J}`.
  Hence `A ∈ served(∏ D_i) ⇒ [A] ∈ Σ Ann_6(D_i)`; `Ann_6` computed mod p is an upper bound, so this is rigorous.

## 2. Files
* `w2lib.py` — library (reducer `Red`, sectors, `e1_kernel`, `Relaxation`, `ReducedE2`, served space, lifting).
* `w2_selftest.py` — controls: accepted witness (`fermat-pair-rational-d4-strict`): Ψ ∈ J and E2' consistent;
  perturbed D inconsistent; lane negative control `F_X0`.

* `w2_ann.py` — annihilator catalogue (sum principle) → `ann_<job>.json`.
* `w2_families.py` — structured candidates d = 8..12: E1, reduced E2' at two primes → `fam_<job>.json`.
* `w2_d9.py` — degree-9 non-Jacobian kernel (21+21 lines): re-verification and individual E2' tests.
