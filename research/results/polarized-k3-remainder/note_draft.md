### 1.2 Equivalence with the generalized Hodge conjecture for Λ²T

Write `V₂ = T ⊕ NS_prim` (rational Hodge structures), so `Λ²V₂ = Λ²T ⊕ (T ⊗ NS_prim) ⊕ Λ²NS_prim`.

correspondences (book §3A.4), and algebraic correspondences preserve the coniveau filtration N¹.

**Proposition.** Let Y = X ∩ V(D) for a legal D and N_Y ⊂ H⁴(X,Q) the image of ⊕ H²(Ỹ_i,Q)(−1) → H⁴(X,Q)
(Ỹ_i resolutions of the components). If the K3 component r of the target has nonzero projection to Λ²T,

### 1.3 Consequences for searching

**(i) Dichotomy.** For every legal D, the K3-sector projection of the (3,1)-targets served by D is either
⊆ ω ⊗ NS_prim (dimension ≤ ρ − 1 ≤ 2; = 0 if ρ = 1) or ⊇ (Λ²T)^{3,1} (dimension ≥ 17). There is no

the K3 projection is 0 for every D tested (§2).

**(ii) Sum principle.** served(D₁D₂) = served(D₁) + served(D₂) modulo J(F) (classes in H^{3,1}). Proof:
by the prescribed-support theorem served(D) = {A : Res ω_A vanishes on X ∖ V(D)} = N_{V(D)∩X} ∩ F³H⁴ in
the τ-invariant part; N_{Y₁∪Y₂} = N_{Y₁} + N_{Y₂} because the image of H⁴_Y(X) → H⁴(X) is the lowest
weight part W₄, which is the image of H²(Ỹ)(−1) for any resolution Ỹ of the components (Deligne), and
Ỹ₁ ⊔ Ỹ₂ resolves Y₁ ∪ Y₂; finally the F³-part of a sum of Hodge substructures is the sum of the F³-parts.
Consequence with the discussion's complete exclusion of degrees ≤ 7: **every product of factors of
degree ≤ 7 is excluded**: f(U)·h, P_i·(cubic), all products of linear forms, all cone products; only
denominators with an irreducible factor of degree ≥ 8 can work. (Caveat: the discussion's exclusion
is for *legal* D; a factor divisible by F is not legal, but then D is not legal either.)

Two further remarks. Every Y-odd element of J(F)_6 is served by every D (write A = Σ c_i ∂_iF with c_i

### 1.4 What the divisor must look like

A smooth hypersurface section Y has H²(Y) ≅ Q (Lefschetz), so V(D) ∩ X must be singular and the needed
classes come from the singularities. The resolution Ỹ must carry, in H²(Ỹ), a weight-2 Hodge structure
containing so(T)(1): h^{2,0} ≥ 19 with Mumford–Tate group SO(T) in the adjoint representation.

* vertex-a-line cones D ∈ k[l₁,…,l₄]: Y is singular at the 6 points of L ∩ X; the exceptional surfaces
  are copies of the base surface S' ⊂ P³ (p_g(S') = C(d−1,3); 35 for octics, 10 for sextics). To reach the
  K3 sector, H²_tr(S') would have to contain so(T)(1): S' would be a "Kuga–Satake surface" for this K3 —
  the book's Σ ⊂ A × A with dim A = 2^{rank T − 2} ≥ 2^{17}; no octic in P³ written down by hand is one.
* vertex-a-plane cones D ∈ k[l₁,l₂,l₃]: Y is singular along the plane sextic Γ = Π ∩ X; the exceptional
  divisor is a C'-bundle over Γ (C' = base curve); the Hodge structure is H¹(Γ) ⊗ H¹(C')(−1), of
  product-of-curves type, whose Mumford–Tate group is a product of symplectic-type groups — it cannot
  contain the adjoint representation of SO(T) unless J(Γ) or J(C') involves the Kuga–Satake abelian
  variety, impossible for plane curves of genus ≤ 21. Π = {Y = 0} gives Γ = C = {f = 0}, the branch sextic
  itself (D = g(Y)); tested (§2) — inconsistent, as predicted.
* Non-isolated singularities along a curve with exceptional divisor E → Γ give H¹(Γ) ⊗ H¹(fibre) again;
  isolated non-cone singularities give exceptional surfaces, i.e. the first case.
* On S₆ × S₆ a D of degree d defines the μ₆-invariant (d,d)-divisor {Σ c_{ab} z^a z'^b u^{|b|} v^{|a|} = 0}:
  the joining divisor is D = f(U) (exceptional divisor over C × C), hyperplanes U_i are {ℓ(z) = 0} × S₆,
  bilinear D = UᵀMV are the (1,1)-correspondences {zᵀMz' = 0}, c(U) − κc'(V) are {u³c'(z') = κ v³c(z)}.
  Graph-type divisors have Gysin image in H²⊗H⁴ ⊕ H⁴⊗H²; families of curves S₆ × C' give T ⊗ NS; neither

## 4. Mathematical ideas developed, partial constructions, what would be needed to finish

4.1 **Sharp reformulation (§1.2).** The instance is equivalent to GHC (coniveau 1) for Λ²T of the degree-2
K3 `w² = f`, with the single loophole η ∈ NS_prim ⊗ C (needs ρ ≥ 2). The two routes to a solve:
 (A) prove Λ²T ⊂ N¹H⁴(S₂×S₂) — by algebraicity of the Kuga–Satake correspondence (no known criterion
     applies: ρ ≤ 3, End_Hdg(T) = Q, no non-symplectic automorphism, no RM/CM, not Kummer/Shioda–Inose or
     six-line) or by a new geometric construction of a divisor on S₂ × S₂ whose resolution carries so(T)(1);
 (B) show ρ ≥ 2 and η ∈ NS_prim ⊗ C (then r lies in the T ⊗ NS sector, which the book supports) — testable
     only by certified period computations (Lairez–Sertöz) of the K3 and of the class of z0²z1²z2²; the
     discussion's finite-field data (one splitting line mod 3, obstructed lift mod 9) make ρ = 1 likely.

4.2 **Sum principle (§1.3 ii)** removes the whole "cone product / structural product" part of the menu and
explains the discussion's spurious necessary points D = f_U h at degree 8 (products of excluded pieces).
Any future degree-8/9 exclusion only needs to treat D with an irreducible factor of degree ≥ 8.

4.3 **Dichotomy (§1.3 i)** gives a decisive diagnostic for any proposed D: K3-projection of the served
space ≥ 17 ⟺ D solves every K3-sector target at once. It also suggests a *strengthened exclusion
system*: a degree-8 D serving this target must serve all 19 targets B(U) − B(V), B ∈ R_{f,6}; adding these
19 right-hand sides to the Koszul-parametrized system multiplies the equations without changing the
315 denominator parameters (though each target needs its own Koszul corrections), and the reducible
locus {f_U h, …} can be removed a priori (4.2).

4.4 **Maximal rank of [Â] (§1.5)**: the E1 kernel does not single out candidates; it is a dimension count.

4.5 **Where a construction would have to come from.** By the exterior-power theorem (book §3A.6) an
algebraic Kuga–Satake class produces a divisor of S₂ × S₂ swept by a family over a surface Σ ⊂ A × A
(dim A = 2^{rank T − 2}); the corresponding D(U,V) would have astronomically large degree. A degree-8 or
10 denominator would need a different mechanism producing a weight-2 Hodge structure of adjoint type
so(T)(1) inside H² of the resolution of a low-degree hypersurface section; the Hodge structures available
from cone/curve singularities (§1.4) are of surface type H²(S') or product-of-curves type and are
unrelated to SO(T) unless S' is itself Kuga–Satake-like. Hyperkähler geometry does not help either:
Λ²H²(S) never occurs in H*(S^[n]) (Göttsche–Soergel: only symmetric powers of the even cohomology appear).
We found no candidate mechanism; this is consistent with every experiment.
