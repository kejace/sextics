## 1. The obstruction and the mechanism a certificate needs

**Setting.** `X = V(F)` is the cyclic three-link sextic (CY fourfold, `h^{4,0}=1`, `h^{3,1}=426`,
`h^{2,2}=1752`). `α = Res(AΩ/F³) ∈ F²H⁴(X)`; its image in `Gr_F² = R_12` (Jacobian ring) is `[A] ≠ 0`
and, by `fqlx`'s pairing computation, its `(3,1)` component is nonzero as well. The diagonal group `G`
(order 72 mod scalars) acts; `K = ker(χ_A)` has index 3 and `α` lies in a nontrivial `μ_3`-isotypic
sector ("`ω`-sector"). Because `H^{4,0}` lies in the trivial sector, the `Q`-sub-Hodge structure
generated by `α` has no `(4,0)` part, so the generalized Hodge conjecture *predicts* `α ∈ N¹H⁴`, i.e.
a certificate exists; nothing in the discussion contradicts this. The difficulty is purely
constructive.

**Fine-sector bookkeeping (computed, this session, `check_served2.py`).** In the fine character
sector of `A` under the full diagonal group (order 432 with scalars): `dim (S_12)_κ = 87–90`
(depending on the generator set), `dim (J_12)_κ = 64`, `dim (R_12)_κ = 26`, `dim (R_6)_κ = 2`
(the previous run's "6" used a different sector convention), and the map `B ↦ [div B]` from
`(J_12)_κ` to `(R_6)_κ` has rank 6 in the solver's sector. Consequently the space of degree-12
numerators of `A`'s character whose residue class is **zero** has dimension 58, and the nonzero-class
part of the sector has dimension **32** (`= 26` from `R_12` `+ 6` from the pole-order-two tail). These
32 dimensions are what a denominator must reach; the statistic `new(D) = 32 − unserved(D)` below
counts how many of them a given `D` reaches. (Verified independently with a no-symmetry flint
computation: `served(D=1) = 58`.)

**Where a `(3,1)` part can come from (book ch.3 §3.1).** `N¹H⁴(X)` is generated by Gysin images of
`H²` of the divisors of an embedded resolution of the support; the `(3,1)` part comes only from
`H^{2,0}` of those divisors. For `Z = X ∩ V(D)` (ample), `H²(O_Z) = 0`; so `p_g(Z̃) > 0` forces
non-normal or non-rational singularities. Two explicit families were analysed here with the exact
sequence `0 → O_{X̃}(−Z̃) → O_{X̃} → O_{Z̃} → 0` and Serre duality
(`H²(O_{Z̃}) ≅ H¹(X̃, O(Z̃ + K_{X̃}))^∨`, `H^i(O_{X̃}) = 0` for `i = 1,2,3`):

* *Line vertex* (`D` a degree-`d` form in 4 coordinates, vertex line `L`, `X ∩ L` = 6 points `P_k`):
  `Z̃ + K_{X̃} = dH − (d−3) Σ E_k`, so `p_g(Z̃) = dim coker( H⁰(O_X(d)) → ⊕_k O_{X,P_k}/m^{d−3} )`.
  Since the tangent space of `X` at `P_k` is spanned by the four cone directions and the line
  coordinate vanishes to order 4 on `X` there, the cokernel is `Σ_{j=0}^{d−4} C(j+3,3)·max(0, 5−d+j)`:
  **`p_g(Z̃) = 0, 1, 4, 10` for `d = 3, 4, 5, 6`, independently of the base surface** (the forms are
  dual to the failure of interpolation of jets at six collinear points by binary forms). So every
  sextic cone with a line vertex (e.g. `F|_{L^⊥}`, the "edge" sextics, Fermat cones) carries ten
  `(2,0)`-forms on `Z̃`; whether their Gysin images are nonzero and in the `ω`-sector is what the
  `D²` tests decide.
* *Plane vertex* (`D` in 3 coordinates, singular along the Fermat sextic curve `C = X ∩ Π`, `g = 10`):
  blowing up `C` gives `Z̃ + K_{X̃} = dH − (d−2)E`, `p_g(Z̃) = dim coker(H⁰(O_X(d)) → H⁰(O_X/I_C^{d−2}(d)))`,
  and because `N_{C/X} ≅ O_C(1)³` and `h⁰(O_C(k)) = h⁰(O_{P²}(k))` for `k ≤ 5`, the map is surjective:
  **`p_g(Z̃) = 0` for every cone of degree ≤ 6 with a plane vertex**, including cubic (elliptic-curve)
  cones. The previous run's heuristic "`C × B` forms" over-counted: those forms live on the exceptional
  surface but do not extend to `Z̃`. Plane-vertex cones cannot serve the `(3,1)` part.

**Fibration picture.** In `hodgepodgeconjecture`'s chart `X` is a family of plane cubics over the
`(v, c_1, c_2)`-base and, after the paired-sign/`τ` quotient, a family of `(3,3)`-surfaces `S` over
the `(c_1,c_2)`-plane with `p_g(S) = 2`, the two forms having `μ_3`-characters `ω, ω²`. `DiamondJM`
showed the `ω`-sector `V_ω` (rank 12, Hodge `(1,10,1)`) is generated by the holomorphic form under
Gauss–Manin and the Kodaira–Spencer map is injective on the 2-dimensional base tangent space. Hence a
flat `(2,0)`-section along a base curve does not exist, so **a vertical divisor `π⁻¹(C)` can only
contribute algebraic `(2,2)` classes** (Noether–Lefschetz classes) and cannot carry `α^{3,1}`;
likewise the hyperplane sections (base locus of the chart) are smooth. A support therefore must
contain a *horizontal* component `Z`, i.e. a family of curves `Γ_c ⊂ S_c`, with `p_g(Z̃) > 0` coming
from non-normality/non-rational singularities of the closure. The known supports on this `X` (the
accepted `cycle-three` proof) are existential precisely because the natural horizontal loci
(Hessian `H = 0`, `J = 0`, discriminant of the Weierstrass model) have very large degree after
clearing the chart; the same should be expected here. Low-degree `D` are a long shot, and the
experiments below are consistent with that.
