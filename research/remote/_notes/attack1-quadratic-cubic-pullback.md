# Attack 1: `quadratic-cubic-pullback` (q = 1, A = X0^3 Y0^3)

(3,1)-space of a denominator from the singular locus of V = X ∩ V(D); it reproduces every computed served space
(Fermat join: 100, deformed joins: exactly ⟨δ⟩, cones: 0) and shows that the Fermat mechanism for this target
(two coordinate-plane sextic curves with transversal sextic cones) is not realisable for this F in any degree,
because F is not a join and F_{42}, F_{24} ≠ 0.  Rigorous: no certificate of degree ≤ 7 exists (E1 kernel = J(F));
the degree-8 kernel computation is reported below when finished.

results are in `log_engine1_unreliable.jsonl` and were all recomputed).  The first run of this attack was killed

## 1. The obstruction and the geometric mechanism a certificate needs

### 1.1 Data
`F = Σ_{i=0}^5 Q_i^3` with the six Y-even quadrics (verified exactly, F − ΣQ_i^3 = 0)

```
Q0 = X0^2 + 5/7 (X1X2 + Y0Y1)     Q3 = Y0^2 + 5/17 (X0X1 + Y1Y2)
Q1 = X1^2 + 5/11(X0X2 + Y1Y2)     Q4 = Y1^2 + 5/19 (X1X2 + Y0Y2)
Q2 = X2^2 + 5/13(X0X1 + Y0Y2)     Q5 = Y2^2 + 5/23 (X0X2 + Y0Y1)
```
φ = (Q_0..Q_5): P^5_Z → P^5_U is finite flat of degree 32 (mod 5 it is the squaring map, so base-point free);
X = φ^{-1}(C), C the Fermat cubic fourfold; f = φ|_X factors through W = X/τ → C (degree 16).
δ = det(∂Q_i/∂Z_j) is Y-odd of degree 6 (64 terms, `delta.txt`); R = V(δ) ∩ X is the ramification divisor.
Bigrading by (X-degree, Y-degree): F = F_{60} + F_{42} + F_{24} + F_{06} with 10, 15, 15, 10 terms; all four
pieces are nonzero.  **F is not a join** G(ℓ) + H(ℓ') for any pair of complementary 3-spaces of linear forms:
the 21 second partials ∂_i∂_jF are linearly independent (a join has 9 vanishing mixed partials, rank ≤ 12)
(`jointest.py`).  The diagonal symmetry group of F is only ⟨τ⟩ (Smith form of the exponent lattice:
`[1,1,1,1,2,0]`), so the only sector decomposition is Y-parity; A is Y-odd = τ-invariant residue.

### 1.2 Hodge-theoretic position of [A]
* h^{3,1}(X)^+ = 226 − 18 = 208 (Y-odd degree-6 monomials minus the 18 Y-odd Jacobian relations Z_j∂_iF with
  one index in each block).  f^*H^{3,1}(C) = ⟨[δ]⟩ (Piola/weighted-Fano, book 3.4).  [A] is independent of [δ]
  mod J(F) (mod 5 they are distinct Fermat monomials X0^3Y0^3 and 4·X0X1X2Y0Y1Y2).
* f_*C_X = C ⊕ L_15 ⊕ M_16 (τ-invariant part C ⊕ L_15, L_15 the standard representation of the 16-sheeted
  cover W → C whose monodromy is generated by transpositions around the irreducible branch divisor).  The
  residual 207-dimensional invariant space is H^4(C, L_15)^{3,1}.  All intransitive quotients of the Galois
  closure (W, the symmetric fibre square, ...) are of general type (K of the fibre square = pr_2^*(R) is big),
  so the quotient-transfer / uniruled-quotient route (book 3.1–3.3, 4.7) is closed; benediamond's comment
  (discussion #175) closes pulled-back line cylinders under a regularity hypothesis.  The fibrations of X
  induced by the quadrics (X ⇢ P^2 via (Q0:Q1:Q2), or pullbacks of cubic-surface fibrations of C) have fibres
  of general type ((2,2,6) and (2,2)∩(6) complete intersections, K = O(4)), so no surface-fibration theorem
  applies.  Only a direct divisor support with non-rational singularities (book 3.1) can work.
* Because the (4,0)-class Res(Ω/F) is τ-anti-invariant, H^4(X,Q)^+ has h^{4,0} = 0 and the generalized Hodge
  conjecture predicts that every τ-invariant residue, including [A], is supported on a divisor; no degree
  bound follows.

### 1.3 Adjoint-condition criterion (the tool developed in this attack)
For V = X ∩ V(D) (D of degree d, V a complete intersection so H^i(O_V(k)) = 0 for 0 < i < 3) with a resolution
ρ: Ṽ → V, the (3,1)-classes supported on V are the Gysin images of H^{2,0}(Ṽ) (book 3.1).  Since H^2(O_V) = 0,
all of H^2(O_Ṽ) comes from the singularities, and by Serre duality + Grauert–Riemenschneider

    h^{2,0}(Ṽ) = h^1(Ṽ, K_Ṽ) = h^1(V, K_V ⊗ I_adj) = h^1(X, I_{Z_adj}(d)),       K_V = O_V(d),

where I_adj = ρ_*O(−(discrepancy)·E) is the adjoint ideal and Z_adj ⊂ X the adjoint subscheme.  With
H^1(O_X(d)) = 0 this is the **failure of Z_adj to impose independent conditions on |O_X(d)|**:

    h^{2,0}(Ṽ) = h^0(O_{Z_adj}(d)) − ( h^0(O_X(d)) − h^0(I_{Z_adj}(d)) ).

Transversal types along a curve Γ ⊂ Sing V and their adjoint ideals (2-dimensional slices in the 3 normal
directions of Γ ⊂ X): cone over a plane curve of degree m has p_g = C(m−1,3), discrepancy 2 − m, adjoint ideal
I_Γ^{m−2}; m = 2 (A_1 along a curve) and all A_k are rational (no conditions); m = 3 simple elliptic
(adjoint = I_Γ); m = 6 (Fermat) adjoint = I_Γ^4.  Isolated singular points of 3-folds (e.g. cone over a quartic
surface, p_g = 1) impose finitely many conditions, which are independent in all cases met here; they never help.
Sector rule (checked on the Fermat join below): the τ-invariant (3,1)-classes (Y-odd numerators) correspond to
the Y-odd sector of the cokernel.

Checks of the criterion:
* Fermat sextic, D = X0^6+X1^6+X2^6: V = join of the two plane sextic curves Γ_Y = X∩{X=0}, Γ_X = X∩{Y=0}
  (transversal sextic cones, adjoint I^4 along both).  Conditions 2·280 = 560, kernel span(G,H)/F of dim 1,
  so h^{2,0} = 560 − 460 = 100, Y-odd sector 326 − 226 = 100, Y-even 0.  The solver gives served dim 118 =
  18 + 100 (log, 408 s) and the strict certificate v = A E_X/(6D) (verified exactly).  Exact match.
* Deformed join D = Q0^3+Q1^3+Q2^3 on our F: V = {Q0^3+Q1^3+Q2^3 = 0 = Q3^3+Q4^3+Q5^3} is singular exactly
  along the two curves C_i = S_i ∩ X (S_1 = {Q3=Q4=Q5=0}, S_2 = {Q0=Q1=Q2=0} smooth K3 surfaces of degree 8;
  C_i ∈ |6H| on S_i, g = 145, K_{C_i} = O(6)) with simple elliptic transversal type (adjoint = I_{C_1 ∪ C_2}).
  H^0(O_X(6)) → H^0(O_{C_i}(6)) is onto (through the K3, H^1(O_{S_i}) = 0) with kernel ⟨Q_S^3⟩ on each side,
  and the joint restriction has cokernel exactly 1 (the Koszul H^2(O_S) term), all in the Y-odd sector.  So
  h^{2,0}(Ṽ) = 1, and this form is the pullback of the unique (2,0)-form of the resolved join of two plane
  cubics in C, whose Gysin image is the (3,1)-class of C.  Prediction: served space = J ⊕ ⟨δ⟩.  Computed:
  served dim 19 = 18 + 1 (target_image, 690 s) and the fixed-D system with target δ is **consistent**

* Cone over a plane cubic with vertex a K3 curve, D = G(Q_a,Q_b,Q_c), G ≠ Q_a^3+Q_b^3+Q_c^3: Sing V = C_{abc}

  `cones012.txt`, `cones345.txt` are predicted dead and were not run (the 3+3 joins are the only members with a
  nonzero served space, and they serve only δ).
* Cones with vertex a plane Π (D = G(ℓ_1,ℓ_2,ℓ_3) sextic in three linear forms, transversal sextic cone along
  the plane sextic Γ = Π ∩ X): single curve, h^1(I_Γ^4(6)) = 0 exactly (plane-curve identity
  h^0(O_Γ(k)) = C(k+2,2) − C(k−4,2)); in fact for a single thickened plane sextic h^1(I_Γ^a(d)) = 0 for all a, d.
  Computed: fermatX = X0^6+X1^6+X2^6 on our F (see table).  Vertex a line (base a sextic surface, p_g = 15):

### 1.4 Why the Fermat mechanism cannot be transported to this F (any degree)
The Fermat certificate needs V singular along two curves with transversal orders (m_1, m_2) so that the adjoint
conditions exceed the sections.  For the coordinate planes, exact computation (`adjcount.py`, both sectors):

| d | (m1,m2) | realisable c-dim | odd: cond / ker / image / bound | even: cond / ker / image / bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 6 | (6,4) | 0 | 226 / 0 / 226 / 0 | 144 / 92 / 144 / 0 |
| 6 | (4,4) | 1 (D = F_{60}+F_{42}) | 126 / 100 / 126 / 0 | 54 / 182 / 54 / 0 |
| 8 | (6,4) | 12 (D = c_{20}(F_{60}+F_{42}) + c_{02}F_{60}) | 408 / 228 / 408 / 0 | 240 / 411 / 240 / 0 |
| 8 | (6,6) | 0 | 618 / 18 / 618 / 0 | 402 / 249 / 402 / 0 |

| 10 | (6,6) | 66 | 930 / 561 / 930 / 0 | 570 / 942 / 570 / 0 |

General statement (proved by the Koszul sequence of I_1 ∩ I_2): for two disjoint thickened curves,
h^1(I_{Z_1∪Z_2}(d)) = dim coker( I_1(d) ⊕ I_2(d) → O_X(d) ), which for the coordinate planes is the number of
monomials of bidegree (k,l), k < m_1−2, l < m_2−2, k+l = d, minus what F·S_{d−6} covers.  Nonzero needs
m_1 + m_2 ≥ d + 6; realisability of such orders needs a Y-even c ∈ S_{d−6} with c·F having no terms in a
window of ≥ 3 consecutive even Y-degrees, which forces c_{20}F_{42} = −c_{02}F_{60} etc., impossible because
F_{60} is an irreducible plane sextic and F_{42} ≠ 0 (nullity 0 of the (4,4)-cancellation system at d = 8;
the equation count grows faster than dim S_{d−6} for larger d).  Any other pair of complementary planes gives
the same obstruction unless F is a join in those coordinates, which it is not (§1.1).  Hence **no denominator
whose only non-rational singularities are transversal cones along plane sections of X can serve [A]**, and the
Fermat mechanism does not deform.

## 4. Mathematical ideas developed

4.1 **Served space = adjoint cokernel.**  For a complete-intersection support V = X ∩ V(D) of degree d,
h^{2,0}(Ṽ) = h^1(X, I_{Z_adj}(d)) = (conditions imposed by the adjoint scheme) − (rank of restriction of
|O_X(d)|), computed sector by sector under τ; the Y-odd sector of the cokernel matches the τ-invariant served
classes (Fermat join: 100/0; deformed join: 1/0; cones: 0/0).  This gives a cheap (seconds) predictor for
degree-8–12 denominators whose solves cost hours, and a design principle: the adjoint scheme must fail to impose
independent conditions on degree-d forms, i.e. it must be *non-ACM at degree d*.

4.2 **Why pulled-back supports only serve δ.**  For the join of the K3 curves, the cokernel is the socle
of the Artinian ring S/(Q_0,…,Q_5) (Hilbert series (1+t)^6, one-dimensional in degree 6), and the socle of
the Jacobian ideal of a regular sequence of quadrics is the Jacobian determinant δ itself.  Any divisor of C
pulled back through the unramified part of f keeps its transversal type; the branch divisor is irreducible and
its corank-2 locus is finite, so no curve of C acquires a worse transversal type upstairs.  Hence pullbacks of
cones/joins from the cubic serve exactly f^*H^{3,1}(C) = ⟨δ⟩.

4.3 **ACM obstruction for cheap curves.**  Every adjoint scheme tried is ACM at the relevant degree: one
thickened plane section (any order, any degree — identity h^0(O_Γ(k)) = C(k+2,2) − C(k−4,2)); two thickened
coordinate plane sections (table §1.4: the cokernel is the span of the "gap" monomials of bidegree (k,l),
k < m_1−2, l < m_2−2, k+l = d, which is empty unless m_1+m_2 ≥ d+6, and such orders are not realisable for
this F); k plane sections Π_i ∩ X lying in a common P^3 (their union is a complete intersection on the sextic
surface P^3 ∩ X, so ACM; moreover simple-elliptic transversal type along k such curves at degree d needs
3k ≤ d, and for k ≥ 3 at d ≤ 8 the denominator is forced into (λ_1,λ_2)^2, making V a double surface whose
normalisation is rational); pairs of K3 curves C_{abc} ∪ C_{abd} meeting in 96 points (the 96 points are the
divisor of F on the genus-17 curve {Q_a=Q_b=Q_c=Q_d=0}, so they impose only 80 conditions and the count is
again exactly tight).  In every case the count is *exactly tight*, i.e. the cokernel vanishes with no slack.

4.4 **The Fermat mechanism does not deform.**  The 100 Fermat classes come from d = 6 being below the
regularity of two order-4 thickened plane sextics; the deformation Q_i = Z_i^2 + 5(…) destroys the
join structure (F_{42}, F_{24} ≠ 0), and no multiple c·F recovers a bigraded gap in any degree (§1.4).  The
Fermat certificate v = A·E_X/(6D) is the subset-Euler field; for Σ Q_i^3 the analogous field would need
a vector field V with V(Q_i) = Q_i for i ∈ S and 0 otherwise, i.e. the Piola fields adj(M)·∂ which have
degree 5 coefficients and produce D = δ·ΣQ_i^2-type denominators (δQ_i^2 ∈ J(F), degree 10): their supports
are R ∪ 2(Q_i = 0), both with rational singularities.

4.5 **Transfer routes.**  f_*C = C ⊕ L_15 ⊕ M_16 with L_15 irreducible (standard representation of the
16-sheeted W → C); all intransitive quotients of the Galois closure are of general type; W = X/τ has κ = 0;
the quadric fibrations of X have fibres of general type.  So the nonconstructive solves of the catalogue
(quotient cover + trace, elliptic companions) have no analogue here.

4.6 **Where a certificate could still come from.**  (i) The E1 kernel becomes nontrivial at d = 10 for
dimension reasons (≥ 294 denominators mod J); a geometric candidate there would be a divisor with a
non-ACM adjoint scheme not of pull-back type — e.g. V singular along a curve lying on a surface S ⊂ X with
H^1(O_S(k)) ≠ 0 or along two curves on different non-ACM surfaces.  Natural surfaces in X that are not
complete intersections (hence possible sources of non-ACM curves): the surfaces f^{-1}(plane cubic surface
cones)?, the corank-1 part of the ramification surface R ∩ V(∂δ), fixed curves Γ_X, Γ_Y of τ with the
exceptional structure of W.  (ii) Bundles: A = Σ A_i with each A_i served by a different D_i (D = ΠD_i);
needs a family of denominators whose served spaces jointly span [A]; all computed served spaces so far lie
in J ⊕ ⟨δ⟩, so no bundle exists from the tested families.
