### 1.3 Symmetry

**Diagonal group [computed, proved].** Substitutions `Z_i ↦ ζ^{g_i} Z_i` (`ζ = e^{2πi/6}`) with
`F∘g = F` form the group of `g ∈ (Z/6)⁶` orthogonal to the exponent-difference lattice `L(F)`
(Chapter 1): 144 elements, i.e. order **24 modulo global scalars**, isomorphic to
`Z/6 × Z/2 × Z/2`, generated by

* `μ6` on `Y2`: `g = (0,0,0,0,0,1)`;
* `ρ`: `(Y0,Y1) ↦ (−Y0,−Y1)`, `g = (0,0,0,3,3,0)`;
* `σ`: `(X1,Y1) ↦ (−X1,−Y1)`, `g = (0,3,0,0,3,0)`.

`τ = (0,0,0,3,3,3) = ρ·r` with `r` the `Y2` reflection `(0,0,0,0,0,3)`. Other sign changes that
are symmetries: `(X0,X1,X2)` (= `τ` mod scalar), `(X0,X2,Y0)` (= `σρ`·X-sign), `(X1,Y0)` (= `σρ`).
This confirms `DiamondJM`'s count (24, versus 216 for `one-reflection`).

**No other monomial symmetry [computed].** Among the 720 coordinate permutations only the identity
and `Y0 ↔ Y1` preserve the monomial support of `F`, and no diagonal rescaling (sixth roots of unity,
global scalar allowed) turns `Y0 ↔ Y1` into a symmetry. So the group of monomial linear symmetries
(permutation × diagonal) is exactly the 24-element diagonal group above.

**No linear symmetry mixing `Y0,Y1` while diagonal elsewhere [proved, inference].** If `g` is
diagonal on `X0,X1,X2,Y2` and linear on the `(Y0,Y1)` plane, the pure `(Y0,Y1)` part of `F∘g` is
`(αY0+βY1)⁶ + (γY0+δY1)⁶` and must equal `Y0⁶ + Y1⁶`. By Sylvester's uniqueness of the rank-2
Waring decomposition of a binary form of degree ≥ 3, `{αY0+βY1, γY0+δY1} = {ζ^iY0, ζ^jY1}`, so `g`
is monomial and hence in the diagonal group. A fully general linear automorphism of `F` has not
been computed by anyone; nobody in the thread claims one exists.

**Rescaled exchanges are impossible [proved].** `DiamondJM`: a twist `X0 ↦ ζ^aX0, X1 ↦ ζ^bX1`
making `Y0 ↔ Y1` a symmetry would need `a + b ≡ 3` and `3a + b ≡ 0 (mod 6)`, impossible by parity.
I re-derived this with arbitrary complex scalars on all six coordinates and `F ↦ κF` allowed: the
conditions force `λ_{X0}² = −η` with `η³ = 1`, hence `λ_{X0}⁶ = −1`, contradicting `X0⁶ ↦ κX0⁶`.
Equivalently: the skew term is odd under the exchange while the Fermat terms are even, and the only
way to compensate the sign is a sixth root of `−1` on a variable that carries a pure sixth power.

**Residue characters [computed].** For a monomial numerator `Z^e` and `g = ζ^w`, the residue of
`Z^eΩ/F^{q+1}` has character `ζ^{⟨w,e⟩ + Σw}` (the `Σw` is the determinant of the volume form).
For `A = X0³Y0³`:

| generator | character of `A` | character of `Ω` | residue character |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Y2 ↦ ζY2` | 1 | `ζ` | **`ζ`** |
| `ρ` | −1 | +1 | **−1** |
| `σ` | +1 | +1 | **+1** |
| `τ` | −1 | −1 | +1 (as required) |
| `r` (`Y2 ↦ −Y2`) | +1 | −1 | **−1** |

So the residue is invariant exactly under `⟨σ, τ⟩ ≅ (Z/2)²`. The surviving coordinate reflection
`r` does **not** serve the target (the residue is `r`-anti-invariant; §3.3 of the book serves the
`r`-invariant residues, i.e. targets odd in `Y2`). `checks.json`: `coordinateReflections = [Y2]`,
`coordinateReflectionsServingTarget = []`, `brokenSymmetries = [Y0/Y1 exchange, negative Y0/Y1 exchange]`.

### 1.4 Block and split structure

* **No paired-sign cubic reduction [computed].** The only two-element sign-change symmetries are
  `{X1,Y1}` and `{Y0,Y1}`; they share `Y1`. Three disjoint pairs, as needed for the paired-sign
  cubic of `cycle-three`/`mixed-biweight` (book §2.9), do not exist.
* **`σ`-linear structure [proved].** With `a = X1²`, `b = Y1²`, `m = X1Y1` (`m² = ab`):
  `F = Φ0 + m·Φ1`, `Φ0 = X0⁶ + X2⁶ + Y0⁶ + Y2⁶ + a³ + b³`,
  `Φ1 = X0³Y0 + X2Y0·a + (1/7)X0Y0³ − (1/7)X0Y0·b`. So `X/σ` is birational to the weighted
  degree-12 hypersurface `Φ0² = ab·Φ1²` in `P(1,1,1,1,2,2)`. The residue descends to `X/σ`, but `σ`
  fixes a codimension-2 locus, so `K_{X/σ} ≡ 0` and no Fano-type support theorem applies.
* **`ρ`-structure.** With `U0 = Y0²`, `U1 = Y1²`, `p = Y0Y1`: `F = Φ0' + p·Φ1'`,
  `Φ0' = G0 + Y2⁶ + U0³ + U1³`, `Φ1' = c + (1/7)X0X1(U0 − U1)`. The residue is `ρ`-anti-invariant,
  so it does not descend to `X/ρ` (same for the Fermat-type cover `T⁶ = Y0⁶ + Y1⁶`, which is just
  `X/ρ` over the Fermat curve).
* **Five-variable reduction (`fringewidth`, stated for `one-reflection`; the proof uses only
  `F = f(x) + Y2⁶` with `f` free of `Y2`, so it applies verbatim here) [proved up to the last step].**
  Average a certificate over `μ6(Y2)` (fixes `F` and `A`); after substituting `Y2⁶ = F − f`
  (changing `K_A` by a multiple of `F²`) the field has the shape `v = (u(x), Y2·a(x))` with
  `x = (X0,X1,X2,Y0,Y1)`. Then `K_A(v) = [A − u(f) + f(div u + a)] + Y2⁶(div u − 5a)` is linear in
  `Y2⁶ = F − f`, and `F² | K_A` forces both brackets to vanish:
  `a = div(u)/5` and `A − u(f) + (6/5)·f·div u = 0`.
  Conversely any such rational `u` gives the strict certificate `(u, Y2·div u/5)`. Thus
  **a certificate exists iff the five-variable "`q = 5/6`" equation `A − u(f) + (6/5) f div u = 0`
  has a rational solution** (poles automatically legal: `F` is monic in `Y2`). `fringewidth` further
  states that Euler normalization in five variables turns this into the strict system
  `N(f) = AD`, `D div N − N(D) = 0`; I could not reproduce that last step (the `6/5`-equation is
  not invariant under five-variable Euler shifts), so I treat the strict five-variable system as
  *sufficient*, the `6/5`-equation as *equivalent*. Geometrically the `6/5`-equation is the
  certificate problem for the multivalued form `AΩ₄/f^{11/6}` on `P⁴ ∖ V`, i.e. for the
  `ζ`-eigenspace of the cyclic cover **[inference]**.
* **Curve fibrations and the CM piece [reported + inference].** (i) Over `(r,w,t)` (`fqlx`): the
  fibre is the genus-4 curve `z⁶ + P(U) = 0`. (ii) Over `(r,w,x = Y0/Y1)` (`DiamondJM`): the fibre
  is the plane sextic `g(X0², Y1²) + Y2⁶ = 0` (genus 10); (i) is its quotient by `X0 ↦ −X0`. In
  both, the eigenspace of `H¹(fibre)` carrying the target's character is **two-dimensional and
  purely of type `(1,0)`** (`DiamondJM`: spanned by `X0³, X0Y1²` times the residue form; `fqlx`:
  `ω0 = dU/z⁵`, `ω1 = U dU/z⁵`, eigenvalue `ζ` under `z ↦ ζz`). I verified the Chevalley–Weil
  count: on `z⁶ = −P(U)` the holomorphic forms are `dU/z³, dU/z⁴, dU/z⁵, U dU/z⁵`, and the
  conjugate eigenvalue `ζ⁵` has no holomorphic form, so the `ζ`-piece has signature `(2,0)`.
  Consequently the relevant variation of Hodge structure is a **rank-2 unitary local system with
  finite monodromy**, the corresponding abelian surface is isotrivial of CM type (isogenous to
  `E_{j=0}²`), and `fqlx`'s four twisted `j = 0` elliptic curves `E_β` over the quartic cover are
  its explicit isogeny factors (`Tr_{B/K} T_β = 6·I₂`). The target `(3,1)` class therefore lives
  in `H³(base, L)` for this local system `L`. `DiamondJM` notes the structure is identical on the
  solved `one-reflection`, so "this fibration neither obstructs nor serves".

### 1.5 Smoothness [proved]

`checks.json` records the maximal-coordinate derivative estimate; the argument is complete. Let
`Z` be a nonzero point and `Z_i` a coordinate of maximal modulus `m`. Then `|∂_i(F − F_Fermat)(Z)|
≤ load_i · m⁵` with loads (sum of absolute coefficients of the mixed part of `∂_iF`)
`(23/7, 30/7, 1, 18/7, 18/7, 0)`, all `< 6 = |∂_i Z_i⁶|/m⁵`. So `∂_iF(Z) ≠ 0` and `V(F)` is smooth.
The same estimate (with the `Y2` column dropped) proves smoothness of the threefold `V = {f = 0}`.
