## 5. Candidate attack plan

Linear-system sizes for a fixed Y-even denominator of degree d (q = 1; exact counts, all coefficients, no symmetry reduction; `deg N_i = d+1`, `deg Q = 2d−6`, identity in degree 2d+6):

| d | N unknowns | Q unknowns | total unknowns | equations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1386 | 60 | 1446 | 10152 |
| 6 | 2376 | 226 | 2602 | 16797 |
| 7 | 3861 | 636 | 4497 | 26532 |
| 8 | 6006 | 1491 | 7497 | 40326 |
| 9 | 9009 | 3080 | 12089 | 59332 |
| 10 | 13104 | 5796 | 18900 | 84903 |
| 11 | 18564 | 10152 | 28716 | 118608 |
| 12 | 25704 | 16797 | 42501 | 162248 |

(Measured: d=6 full solve ≈ 4 min, d=7 colon-only ≈ 8 min on 40 cores with the compressed-modular engine; d=8 full solve extrapolates to ≈ 30–60 min; with the Z/6 h-grading every block shrinks ≈ 6×.) The prescribed-support theorem (Chapter 1) says existence depends only on V(D)_red, so one should test squarefree D's and never products with repeated factors at q=1.

**E1. Exhaust the degree-6 and degree-7 colon spaces with structured members [cheap, decisive for low degree].** The 20-dim (d=6) and 63-dim (d=7) spaces (J:A)/J are saved. Decompose them into G-characters (h-weight mod 6, r2, s parities); for each character the G-semi-invariant denominators form a small linear space; test (i) every basis element, (ii) random combinations mod p, (iii) all members of the form D_H(core)+c·D_B(X2,Y2), (iv) members that are products of lower-degree forms (sweep c in the pencils). A consistent modular system at any D gives an explicit rational witness by CRT lifting (the engine does this). Chance of success: low (≈ 5–10%) — the sector is residual and the two natural members (H, B) fail — but it is the only route to an *explicit* small witness and costs an hour.

**E2. h-graded (X2,Y2)-weight decomposition for core-only denominators [structural, may yield a proof of impossibility for that family].** For D = D_H(X0,X1,Y0,Y1) (no X2,Y2), write N_i and Q in (X2,Y2)-degree k = 0,…. The weight-0 part of E = F²Q is the 4-variable identity `A D² − D Σ_{core} N_i ∂_iH + H(D div_core N − N(D)) = H² Q_0` — a q=1 certificate problem for the *surface* S_H = {H=0} ⊂ P³ with target the (2,0)-type residue of A·Ω_3/H² (deg A = 6 = 2·6 − 6… for a surface in P^3 the (2,0) residues have numerator degree 6−4 = 2, so A of degree 6 is a (1,1)-level numerator of the pole-order-2 form: Res(A Ω/H²) ∈ F^1H^2(S_H)). Since F^1-classes on a surface with q=0 can be divisor-supported only if they are (1,1), one gets: a core-only D exists ⇒ the residue of A on S_H is algebraic (NS-class) — a checkable condition (Picard rank of S_H via point counts / is X0^3Y0^3 in the span of algebraic classes?). Expected outcome: a rigorous **exclusion of all core-only denominators** unless S_H has an unexpectedly algebraic class. Half a day.

**E3. The join/suspension recipe as an algebraic construction [the main theoretical route].** Target: H^{2,1}(T_H)_ζ ⊗ H^{1,0}(P)_{ζ²}. Ingredients that are *already* supported by book theorems: (a) H^4(W_H) for the Fano fourfold W_H = {H+s³+t³=0} ⊂ P(1,1,1,1,2,2) (Chapter 3 covering curves; −K=O(2), degree bound for curves −K·C ≤ 2·4 = 8 in the normal case; its singular locus is the weighted P¹ {X=0} ∩ W_H = {s³+t³=0} = 3 points with ½(1,1,1,1) singularities) — this gives [H^3(T_H) ⊗ H^1(E_F)]^{μ3} divisor-supported; (b) the Kuga–Satake-algebraic K3 surface W_B (order-3 non-symplectic automorphism (s,t)→(ζs,t) with fixed curve C_B — to be double-checked: W_B={B(x,y)+s³+t³=0}⊂P(1,1,2,2) is quasi-smooth; check it is a K3 after resolving the ½-points). Needed: a correspondence Γ ⊂ (T_H × C_B) × (W_H × W_B) (or × a product with E_F) realizing the identification H^1(C_B)_{ζ²} ≅ H^1(E_F)_{ζ²} ⊗ (something algebraic). Since P is simple of dimension 3 this cannot be done curve-to-curve; it must go through H^2(W_B) (which contains H^1(C_B)⊗H^1(E_F) μ3-invariants: W_B birational to (C_B × E_F)/μ3 exactly as in 3A.2, so H^2(W_B)_tr ⊇ [H^1(C_B) ⊗ H^1(E_F)]^{μ3}, a rank-8 piece of type (1,6,1)… check: (1,0)_{ζ²}⊗(1,0)_ζ gives the (2,0) of the K3; indeed one (2,0)). Then H^{2,1}(T_H)_ζ ⊗ H^{1,0}(C_B)_{ζ²} ⊗ H^{0,1}(E_F)_ζ… the dimensions suggest the construction "W_H ×_{E_F} W_B with the translated diagonal" (3A.3 verbatim with T_g replaced by C_B) supports exactly [H^3(T_H)⊗H^1(C_B)]^{μ3} **provided W_B's piece [H^1(C_B)⊗H^1(E_F)]^{μ3} ⊂ H^2(W_B) is divisor-supported on W_B** — for a surface that means algebraic, i.e. of type (1,1), which is false (it contains the (2,0) class). So the literal 3A.3 contraction fails exactly because W_B is K3 and not Fano — this is why.md's statement. The repair must be a correspondence of higher degree: e.g. use W_B × W_B (where the Kuga–Satake algebraicity gives support of Λ²H²(W_B)_tr in codimension ≥ 1 by the §3A.6 exterior-power theorem) — a product fourfold (T_H × C_B)… the degrees do not match naively. **Recommended concrete sub-task**: compute the Hodge structure of W_B (Picard rank via point counts over F_p, transcendental rank) and of the rank-8 piece, then decide whether the §3A.6 exterior-square mechanism can be bent to a (threefold)×(curve) situation. Chance that a complete nonconstructive argument emerges: low-to-moderate (20%); it is the only route to an all-sector theorem for 4+2 splits.

**E4. Core-side specialness.** Compute (mod several p) the zeta function of the sextic surface S_H ⊂ P³ (10 monomials) and its Picard number, and the point counts of T_H. If S_H has Picard rank ≥ 2 with an extra algebraic class in the right character, E2's obstruction disappears and a core-only or core-dominated denominator becomes plausible (the extra divisor class on S_H gives a *candidate denominator*: its equation D_H(X0,X1,Y0,Y1) — exactly the kind of structural denominator missing from the screen). Cost: a few hours (surface over F_p with p ≤ 50: direct counting of H(x)=0 is trivial; the Picard rank bound needs counts over F_{p^k}, k ≤ 11, or the Artin–Tate approach). Chance of producing a witness: 10–15%, but high information value.

**E5. Deformation from the decoupled limit (E2a-type continuation).** F_c = f + g + c·K + B, c ∈ [0,1]. At c = 0 the §2a binary-block theorem gives explicit strict certificates for A = X0³Y0³ (profile (3,3,0): the "two block cubics" packet, D = r_X² + r_Y² built from contact presentations f = C_X³ + r_X², g = C_Y³ + r_Y² (40 presentations each), N = J_X J_Y e, degree-6 denominators). Take the c=0 witness, and attempt to continue: (i) test the c=0 denominators at c=1 (they are 24-ish degree-6 forms over the finite étale cover: D ∈ Q(roots)[X0,X1,Y0,Y1] — these are *exactly* members of the colon space at c=0 but not necessarily at c=1); (ii) solve the fixed-D system at c=1 with D ranging over the 1-parameter deformations r_X² + r_Y² + c·(correction) found by first-order perturbation. Chance: low (10%) — the coupling is not small (5/7, 10/7) — but cheap given the §2a formulas are explicit (`three-block-rational-d4-strict`, `tie-*` give templates).

**E6. Diagonalize B and use the three-plane structure.** b(u,v) = u³+(15/7)u²v+(10/7)uv²+v³ factors over a cubic extension as ∏(u − ρ_i v); so B = ∏_{i}(X2² − ρ_i Y2²) = ∏ (X2 − √ρ_i Y2)(X2 + √ρ_i Y2): the binary block is a product of 6 linear forms ℓ_1…ℓ_6 (the 6 branch points of C_B → P¹). Denominators built from these (e.g. D = ℓ_1ℓ_2ℓ_3·(cubic in core) or the discriminant-type invariants) are the natural analogue of `eight-plane-configuration`'s contact arrangements and were never tested. Also the cone over the plane sextic {ℓ_1…ℓ_6 = 0} ⊂ P¹… trivial. Chance: low (5%), cheap.

**E7. Change the target within the sector.** The χ_A-sector has 32 (3,1)-classes; the thread's framework (and the book §4.4 "one generic nonzero target suffices") suggests testing whether *any* numerator in the sector (e.g. X0³Y0³ + λ·X0Y0³Y1²-type combinations, or the full target-image computation `target_image(D)` of the engine, which returns the space of all targets served by a given D) is served by the structural denominators: run `image_sector(D)` for D ∈ {H, B, colon members} and record `sector_served_mod_J`. If some D serves a *nonzero* element of the sector, the support exists in the sector and the problem becomes finding the right D for A (the sector is irreducible under G only as a direct sum of 1-dim characters, so this does not immediately give A). Cost: minutes per D (functions already exist in `h.py`). Information value high.

**E8. Higher degree random/structured sweeps on the gcloud box (128 cores).** d = 8, 9 with G-semi-invariant squarefree products of the natural blocks (f, g, B, K, ℓ_i, partials of H, Hessians of f, g, b, the discriminant of the pencil u³ + (15/7)u²v + …), all tested mod one prime, consistent ones lifted. ~500 candidates per degree, ≈ 1 day of compute. Chance: 5–10%.

**E9. Prove nonexistence for a family (negative result worth posting).** Using E2: rigorous proof (via Picard rank of S_H) that no denominator depending only on the core variables exists; and via the (X2,Y2)-weight grading, that no D = D_H + c·D_B exists at d ≤ 7. This would be the first rigorous statement in the thread beyond the character computation.

**E10. Bolognesi–Laterveer on W_B (theory).** Verify the claim in §4 item 6 (W_B is a K3 with a non-symplectic order-3 automorphism (s,t)→(ζs,t) whose fixed locus is the genus-4 curve C_B ⊂ W_B) and then see whether the resulting algebraic Kuga–Satake correspondence for W_B, combined with the Fano support of W_H, can be assembled into support for [H^3(T_H)⊗H^1(C_B)]^{μ3}. This is the only path towards an **all-target 4+2 theorem**; the dimension bookkeeping (E3) is the first thing to settle.

## 6. Feasibility: explicit witness vs. nonconstructive argument; Lean requirements

* **Explicit witness.** Everything accepted with an explicit witness in this repository has d ≤ 6 (block-cube, two-block, cone-ladder) or comes from a Nambu/contact atom. Here d ≤ 5 is rigorously excluded (modulo one prime) by the colon condition, and the natural d = 6 members fail. If a witness exists at d = 6–8 it would be found by E1/E6/E8 in hours–days; the engine then produces `witness.json` and `leangen.py` produces the accepted Lean template (`Witness.lean` ≈ 20 KB for d=6; the kernel check scales with the number of terms — at d=8 the cleared identity has ≈ 40k monomials and Lean's `ring`/`decide` approach used by the template may need the chunked normalisation used in the larger accepted witnesses). Probability that a witness of degree ≤ 8 exists: my estimate ≤ 20%, given that the sector is "residual" by design and the class geometrically involves a simple abelian threefold (supports of (3,1) classes must be non-normal or non-rational-singular threefolds in X; the natural candidates — cones over S_H-related surfaces, binary-block cylinders — are exactly the ones in the screen).
* **Nonconstructive argument.** The accepted nonconstructive proofs (`holdout`, `reflection-coupled-fano`, `cyclic-triple-fano`, `crossed-ternary-asymmetric`, `split-ternary-q2`) all follow the pattern: a Fano (or uniruled) auxiliary variety with an explicit polynomial curve family (finite-field seed + invertible incidence Jacobian), polynomial integration, finite trace, physical transport, legal clearing — all formalised with the shared `HodgeComputer/Constructions/{HoldoutCover,HigherResidues}` libraries. For this instance the auxiliary variety would have to carry H^1 of the simple Prym P. The Fano W_H handles the core; the binary side needs a new idea (E3/E10). Until a geometric support theorem for [H^3(T_H)⊗H^1(C_B)]^{μ3} exists on paper, no Lean route exists. If E3/E10 produce a paper proof via a K3 Kuga–Satake correspondence, formalising it is far beyond the current library (it would need algebraic cycles on K3 × abelian varieties); so a nonconstructive Lean proof is realistic only if the argument reduces to curve families on Fano/uniruled varieties plus elliptic/trace calculus — i.e. only if some *uniruled* variety dominating the Prym piece is found.
* **What a Lean proof needs in either case**: the protected statement `HodgeComputer.HasCertificate F A 1` with the exact `instance.rec` polynomials; for a witness: `D ≠ 0`, homogeneity/degrees, `IsCoprime F D` (the accepted template proves coprimality by exhibiting a point/evaluation), and the polynomial identity; for a construction: the irreducibility of F (Eisenstein specialization as in the accepted PRs, e.g. X1=X2=Y1=Y2=0 gives X0^6 + Y0^6 + (5/7)X0^5… one needs a clean Eisenstein slice — e.g. set X0=X1=Y1=0, Y2 = i, X2 = T−1: Y0^6 + (T−1)^6 − 1 as in PR #150), finite-field seed matrices, and the trace/integration library.
* **Overall**: this lane is primarily a *theory* lane (the 4+2 analogue of the 3+3 split theorem is missing and the simple Prym shows the missing piece is genuinely new), with a cheap-to-run but low-probability explicit-witness side. The most valuable near-term contributions to post: (1) the absolute simplicity of the Prym (new, rigorous up to standard reduction theory, with L-polynomials); (2) the E2/E9 core-only exclusion; (3) the sector dimensions (32,80,32) and the h²-uniruled complementary sector; (4) the results of E1/E7.
