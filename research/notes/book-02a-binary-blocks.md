## 1. The theorem of the chapter

**Theorem (2a, main).** Let

    F = P0(X0,X1) + P1(Y0,Y1) + P2(X2^2, Y2^2),

`P0, P1` binary sextics, `P2` a binary cubic (so the third block is a binary sextic in `(X2,Y2)` even
in each of `X2`, `Y2` separately), coefficients in an *effectively presented characteristic-zero
field* `k`, and `F=0` smooth. Then for `q ∈ {1,2}` and **every** `Y`-odd `A` of degree `6q` there
is a **constructive strict** certificate (after Euler normalization if necessary). Finite trace gives a
certificate over `k`. **"No uniform denominator-degree bound is asserted."** [proved in chapter,
modulo the Chapter-2 inputs listed in §7 and §8.]

Hypotheses actually used, itemized:

* H1 (block structure): additive split into three *binary* blocks `V0=(X0,X1)`, `V1=(Y0,Y1)`,
  `V2=(X2,Y2)`; the first two are *same-parity* blocks (all-`X`, all-`Y`), arbitrary sextics.
* H2 (reflections in the mixed block only): `F` is even in `X2` and in `Y2` separately. This is the
  only symmetry hypothesis; it is used solely through Chapter 2's two-reflection cubic-surface
  theorem for the profiles `(2,2,2)` and `(6,0,0)` at `q=1` (and their `q=2` liftings). `P0, P1` need
  **no** symmetry ("arbitrary binary sextics need not have the reflections required by the
  cubic-surface construction", Chapter 4).
* H3 (smoothness ⟺ squarefreeness): `F` smooth ⟺ all three sextics `P0, P1, P2(X2^2,Y2^2)`
  are squarefree; for the third block this means the two endpoint coefficients and the discriminant
  of the cubic `P2(u,v)` are nonzero. [proved in chapter, elementary.]
* H4 (field): effectively presented, char 0; the constructions pass through the finite extension
  generated by contact presentations (§4) and come back by normalized trace (Chapter 1).

Pole orders served: `q=1` and `q=2` only (the theorem is stated for these; Chapter 4's table row
lists `q` = "1,2" for this family, versus "every positive q" for the other families).

Chapter-4 bookkeeping for this family (projective normal form / `GL3×GL3`-saturation / quotient
dimension): **17 / 25 / 8**; coefficients `7+7+4=18`, preserved by a 10-dimensional subgroup, exactly
8 Fermat orbit derivatives outside the family, saturated cone dimension 26. The ambient quotient of
`Y`-even sextics has dimension 218, so this family is codimension 210 there.

## 2. §2a.1 The elementary fields (all strict, all explicit)

### 2.1 The contact field of one block

Suppose a binary sextic block admits a *contact presentation*

    P = C^3 + r^2,   deg C = 2,  deg r = 3   (binary forms in the block variables (x,y)).

Define

    J = {C, r} = C_x r_y - C_y r_x           (a binary cubic; J ≠ 0 when P is squarefree)
    e = r · ( -C_y ∂_x + C_x ∂_y ) / (2J)     (rational vector field in the block)

Identities [proved in chapter, verified here]:

    e(C) = 0,      e(r^2) = r^2,      div(J e) = J/2.

(The Hamiltonian field `-C_y∂_x + C_x∂_y` of `C` is divergence-free and sends `r` to `J`.)
`J ≠ 0`: otherwise `C, r` algebraically dependent and `P` not squarefree.

### 2.2 Packet fields, pole order 1

Let `E_j = u∂_u + v∂_v` be the Euler field of a *different* block `j`, and `P_j` that block's sextic.
Put

    e = e_i + E_j/6,        D = r_i^2 + P_j     (a sextic; coprime to F because block k is absent)

Then `e(F) = e(D) = D` and `div(J_i e) = 5 J_i/6`. For any cubic multiplier `M` with `e(M) = M/6`,

    v = J_i · M · e / D      is strict for  A = J_i M   (q = 1).

Proof: `div(J_i M e) = J_i M`, and differentiating `1/D` cancels it (`e(D) = D`). The two
multipliers used:

| `M`          | target `A = J_i M`            | block profile `(d_i, d_j, d_k)` | name in chapter |
|--------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------------|-----------------|
| `C_i L_j`    | `(C_i J_i) · L_j`             | `(5,1,0)`                       | `C·J` times linear |
| `L_j Q_k`    | `J_i · L_j · Q_k`             | `(3,1,2)`                       | raw `J` times (linear, quadratic) |

(`L_j` linear in block `j`, `Q_k` quadratic in block `k`, `i,j,k` distinct.)

Explicit numerators [verified here], in block positions `(x_i, y_i, u_j, v_j, s_k, t_k)`:

    N = ( -M r_i C_{i,y}/2,  M r_i C_{i,x}/2,  J_i M u/6,  J_i M v/6,  0, 0 ),   deg N = 7,  D = r_i^2 + P_j.

### 2.3 Two decomposed blocks, pole order 1 and 2

With presentations in blocks `i` and `j`: `e = e_i + e_j`, `D = r_i^2 + r_j^2`, `A = J_i J_j`
(profile `(3,3,0)`): `e(F)=e(D)=D`, `div(Ae)=A`, hence `A e/D` is strict for `A` at `q=1`.

    N = ( -J_j r_i C_{i,y}/2,  J_j r_i C_{i,x}/2,  -J_i r_j C_{j,v}/2,  J_i r_j C_{j,u}/2,  0, 0 ),  deg 7.

**q=2 first-integral product field.** For any homogeneous degree-six first integral `β`
(`e(β)=0`), the field

    v = β · (J_i J_j) · e / (2 D)     is strict for  β J_i J_j   at q = 2,

with the **same sextic denominator `D = r_i^2 + r_j^2` at first power** (not squared); numerator
degree 13. The packet used: `β = C_i C_j Q_k`, giving `A = (C_i J_i)(C_j J_j) Q_k`, profile
`(5,5,2)` and permutations. [proved in chapter, verified here.]

### 2.4 Polynomial (denominator-free) packets

`L_j ∂_x` and `L_j ∂_y` (block-`i` partials with a passive linear multiplier from block `j`) serve
`L_j P_{i,x}` and `L_j P_{i,y}` strictly with `D = 1` (divergence zero since `L_j` is in another block).

### 2.5 Block (subset) Euler — imported from Chapter 2 §2.2

For a block `I` (here `|I| = 2`) with `E_I^2(F) = 6 E_I(F)` (true: `E_I(F) = 6 P_I`) and
`deg_I A + |I| = 6`, i.e. `deg_I A = 4`:

    v = A E_I / E_I(F) = A E_I / (6 P_I)     strict at q = 1,   D = P_I  (the block sextic itself).

[my remark, verified here for q=2] the same works at every `q`: `v = A E_I/(6 q P_I)` is strict
whenever `deg_I A = 4`, since `div v = (deg_I A + |I| - 6) A/(q E_I F)`. The chapter uses this at
`q=2` ("any `d_i = 4` is served by block Euler").

Parity: "The fields can be averaged for global `Y`-parity and traced in their coefficient fields."

## 3. §2a.2 The forty-sheet contact cover (the finite cover of the chapter)

**Statement [proved in chapter].** For every squarefree binary sextic `P`, the presentations
`P = C^3 + r^2` (`deg C = 2`, `deg r = 3`, `C, r` homogeneous, double roots of `C` and all
degree-drop charts included), modulo `C ↦ ζ3 C`, `r ↦ -r`, form a **finite étale cover of degree
40**; keeping the normalizations, degree **240**.

Geometry: `C_P : w^2 = P` genus two. For `0 ≠ T ∈ Jac(C_P)[3]`, `L = K ⊗ T` has a unique section
`s`; `s^3 ∈ H^0(K^3) = Sym^3 H^0(K) ⊕ k·w`, so `s^3 = r + a w` with `a ≠ 0`; multiplying by the
hyperelliptic conjugate gives `P = C^3 + r^2` with `C = s·ι(s)` (after sign/cube-root
normalization). Conversely `(w - r)(w + r) = C^3`, `gcd(C,r)=1`, gives `div(w-r) = 3D`, and
`O(D) ⊗ K^{-1}` is nonzero 3-torsion; `r ↦ -r` is `T ↦ -T`. Count: `[3]` is étale of degree
`3^4 = 81`; remove zero, divide by the free inversion: `80/2 = 40`.

**Étaleness, explicitly (the "cover coordinates").** Coordinates are the `3 + 4 = 7` coefficients
of `(C, r)`, matching the 7 coefficients of `P`. The differential of the presentation map is

    (δC, δr) ↦ 3 C^2 δC + 2 r δr.

If zero, `gcd(C,r)=1` forces `r | δC`, impossible for `deg δC = 2 < 3`, so `δC = δr = 0`: invertible
differential at every presentation. "This also proves the coefficientwise lifting used later"
(formal lifts of a presentation along any deformation of `P`; used in §8 for the raising identity).

[my remark] The field generated by one presentation is contained in the 3-torsion field of
`Jac(w^2=P)`, Galois over `k` with group inside `GSp_4(F_3)` (order 103680; 51840 if `μ_3 ⊂ k`).
The chapter gives no bound on the field degree or on the denominator degree after trace.

## 4. §2a.3 Raw moment theorem: raw Wronskians span all cubics

**Theorem [proved in chapter; key determinant verified here].** For every squarefree `P`, the cubics
`J = {C, r}` over the contact cover span `Sym^3` of the block variables (dimension 4).

### 4.1 Flex construction (explicit, reusable)

Rescale one presentation to `P = U^3 - V^2` (i.e. `U = C`, `V = √-1·r`). The plane cubic

    E :  z^3 - 3 U z + 2 V = 0        (coordinates (z, x, y))

is smooth (a singular point would force `z^2 = U`, `V = z^3`, hence `dP = 0`), and `P` is its
discriminant up to a scalar (`disc = 108 P`). For a flex tangent `z = ξ` (`ξ` binary linear) the
restriction of `E` to the tangent is a cube `η^3 = ξ^3 - 3 U ξ + 2 V`. For each `ζ^3 = 1`:

    C_ζ = U - ξ^2 - ζ ξ η - ζ^2 η^2
    S_ζ = η^3 + 2 ζ^2 η^2 ξ + 2 ζ η ξ^2 + ξ^3 - 2 ζ η U - ξ U
    C_ζ^3 + (3/4) S_ζ^2 = P          [verified here, mod ζ^3 = 1, using 2V = η^3 - ξ^3 + 3Uξ]

so `(C_ζ, (√3/2) S_ζ)` are contact presentations (three per flex). Their Wronskians:

    {C_ζ, S_ζ} = -2 {U, V}/3 + ζ R,
    R = (ξ^2 - 2U) {U, η} - ξ η {U, ξ} + 2 {ξ, η} V          [verified here]

Hence every flex contributes the direction `R` (plus `{U,V}`) to the span of raw Wronskians.

### 4.2 Hesse chart (explicit polynomial identity for rank 4)

Over an algebraic closure, put `E` in Hesse form `X^3 + Y^3 + Z^3 - 3κ XYZ` (Artebani–Dolgachev,
Lemma 1) with projection center `O = (a:b:1)`; `O ∉ E` and `O` on no flex tangent (otherwise `P`
would have a multiple root). Write

    e  = a^3 + b^3 + 1 - 3κab                       (= E(O) ≠ 0)
    L  = (a^2 - κb) x + (b^2 - κa) y
    q0 = 3a x^2 + 3b y^2 - 3κ xy,     c0 = x^3 + y^3
    Ū  = L^2 - e q0/3
    V̄  = e^2 c0/2 - e q0 L/2 + L^3
    U = Ū/e^2,   V = V̄/e^3.

[verified here] these are exactly the depressed-cubic coefficients: with `(X,Y,Z) = (x + a s, y + b s, s)`
one has `E = e s^3 + 3L s^2 + q0 s + c0`, and `s = z - L/e` gives `z^3 - 3Uz + 2V`.

For a flex `p = (p0:p1:p2)` let `(t0,t1,t2) = ∇E(p)`, `T = t0 a + t1 b + t2` (flex tangent at `O`,
nonzero), and

    ξ̄  = T L - e (t0 x + t1 y)                      (ξ = ξ̄/(eT))
    η   = (p1 - b p2) x - (p0 - a p2) y              (unnormalized)
    R̄_p = (ξ̄^2 - 2 Ū T^2) {Ū, η} - ξ̄ η {Ū, ξ̄} + 2 T {ξ̄, η} V̄      (= e^4 T^2 · R up to the η scale)

[verified here] `ξ̄^3 - 3ŪT^2ξ̄ + 2V̄T^3` is a scalar multiple of `η^3` for the three flexes used.

Let `M` be the `4×4` coefficient matrix (basis `x^3, x^2y, xy^2, y^3`) of
`{Ū,V̄}, R̄_(0:1:-1), R̄_(0:1:-ζ), R̄_(1:0:-1)`, `ζ^2+ζ+1 = 0`. With

    l1 = a+b+κ,      l2 = a+ζb+ζ^2κ,   l3 = a+ζ^2b+ζκ,
    l4 = aκ+ζb+ζ^2,  l5 = bκ+ζ^2a+ζ,   l6 = bκ+ζa+ζ^2,
    l7 = aκ+b+1,     l8 = aκ+ζ^2b+ζ,   l9 = bκ+a+1,

    det M = 8748 (ζ+2) e^5 l1 l2 l3 l4 l5 l6 l7^4 l8^4 l9^4      (8748 = 4·3^7)

[verified here exactly, sign + with the stated row/column order]. The nine `l_i` are the nine
flex-tangent evaluations at `O` (my pairing: `l7,l8,l4 ↔ (0:1:-1),(0:1:-ζ),(0:1:-ζ^2)`;
`l9,l5,l6 ↔ (1:0:-1),(1:0:-ζ),(1:0:-ζ^2)`; `l1,l3,l2 ↔ (1:-1:0),(1:-ζ:0),(1:-ζ^2:0)`), so every
factor is nonzero: rank 4 in the Hesse chart. Row contents observed [my observation, Singular]: `{Ū,V̄}` is divisible by `e^2`, and
`R̄_(0:1:-1)` by `e · l7^3` (`l7 = T/3` for that flex; the other two flex rows were not factored). "Projective changes of the plane and linear changes of the
projection pencil preserve the span rank" [asserted, plausible: `Sym^3` is preserved and Wronskians
scale by determinants].

## 5. §2a.4 Quintic moment theorem (the `C·J` packet)

**Theorem [proved in chapter].** For every squarefree binary sextic `P` over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic zero, the forty classes `C{C,r}` span the 4-dimensional quotient
`S_5/⟨P_x, P_y⟩`; with `P_x, P_y` they span all binary quintics (dimension 6).

### 5.1 Residue duality (explicit formulas)

`R = S/(P_x, P_y)` is a complete intersection of two quintics, socle degree 8, residue pairing
`R_5 ≅ R_3^* = S_3^*`. From

    (P_x, P_y)^T = [[2 r_x, 3 C_x], [2 r_y, 3 C_y]] · (r, C^2)^T,   det = -6 J     [verified here]

and the transformation law for complete-intersection residues:

    Res_{P_x,P_y}(B · C · J) = - Res_{r, C^2}(B · C) / 6,     B ∈ S_3.

For `C = a(t-α)(t-β)` the functional is, up to a fixed nonzero scalar,

    ℓ_{C,r}(B) = [ B(α) r(β) - B(β) r(α) ] / [ a (α-β) r(α) r(β) ]
               = Σ_{C(t)=0} B(t) / ( r(t) C'(t) ),
    α = β limit:  [ B'(α) r(α) - B(α) r'(α) ] / [ a r(α)^2 ]   (roots at infinity included).

Kernel of `ℓ_{C,r}` on `S_3`: `⟨r⟩ + C·S_1`, dimension 3. Denominators nonzero since `gcd(C,r)=1`.

### 5.2 The global divisor of a missing moment

If `0 ≠ B ∈ S_3` annihilated all forty `C·J` classes: on `H × H` (`H: w^2 = P`) the antisymmetric
section `s_B(p,q) = B(p) w(q) - B(q) w(p)` of `3K ⊠ 3K`, divided by the diagonal, descends to
`H^(2) = Sym^2 H`. Classes on `H^(2)` (`x` = class of `{p0 + q}`, `p0` Weierstrass; `δ` diagonal;
`E` = exceptional curve of the Abel map `a : H^(2) → A = Jac H`, the blowup of 0):

    class(s_B/Δ) = 6x - δ/2,   K_{H^(2)} = 2x - δ/2 = E,   E^2 = -1,   a^*Θ = x + E,
    6x - δ/2 = 4 a^*Θ - 3E.

Push-forward: a nonzero effective divisor `Z_B ⊂ A` of class `4Θ`, so `Z_B · Θ = 8`. Every nonzero
`T ∈ A[3]` lies on `Z_B`: its divisor `D = p + q` comes from a presentation `(C, r)` with `w = r` at
the roots `α, β` of `C`, so `s_B(p,q) = 0 ⟺ ℓ_{C,r}(B) = 0` (double support uses the tangent
formula; `w' = r'` on `C = 0`). Thus `Z_B ⊇ A[3] \ {0}` (80 points).

### 5.3 Torsion-intersection bound (reusable lemma)

Let `G = A[3]` (81 points), `A` indecomposable principally polarized, `Z` effective with
`Z·Θ ≤ 8`. Every irreducible component `E` has `a = E·Θ ≥ 2`. If `E^2 = 0`, `E` is an elliptic
translate containing 0 or 9 points of `G`. If `E^2 > 0`, with `m = |E ∩ G|`, `h = |Stab(E) ∩ G|`:

    m^2 ≤ h m + (81 - h) E^2,   h | m,   E^2 ∈ 2Z_{>0},   E^2 ≤ a^2/2,   h ≤ E^2/2,  h ∈ {1,3,9}.

| a | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| max points of G | 13 | 18 | 25 | 31 | 39 | 45 | 51 |

[verified here.] Partitions of 8: `2+2+2+2, 2+2+4, 2+3+3, 2+6, 3+5, 4+4, 8` give bounds
`52, 51, 49, 52, 49, 50, 51` [verified here]; multiplicities only overcount. So `≤ 52 < 80`:
contradiction; no missing `B`; the `C·J` packet spans `R_5`. ∎

## 6. §2a.5 Completion at `q = 1` (finite block-degree bookkeeping)

Profiles `(d0, d1, d2)` = block degrees of a target monomial, sum 6. Serving rule (exhaustive):

| profile (up to permutation) | served by | denominator |
|---|---|---|
| some `d_i = 4` | block Euler `A E_i/(6P_i)` | `P_i` (block sextic) |

| `(3,2,1)` | raw-`J` × `L_j Q_k` packet | `r_i^2 + P_j` |
| `(2,2,2)` | mixed quadratic forced to be `X2 Y2` by `Y`-oddness → Chapter 2 two-reflection theorem | cubic-surface (tritangent) denominators |
| `(6,0,0)` | only globally odd targets are entirely in the mixed block with both `X2`, `Y2` exponents odd → two-reflection theorem | same |

Dimensions of globally odd monomials (parity condition `d1 + deg_{Y2} A` odd) [verified here]:

| packet | dim |
|---|---|

| quintic × passive linear | 48 |

(226 = all `Y`-odd sextic monomials; the Jacobian part in degree 6 is 18, leaving the 208

## 7. §2a.6 Completion at `q = 2`

### 7.1 Jacobian-ring bookkeeping [verified here]

Binary Jacobian ring `k[x,y]/(P_x,P_y)`: Hilbert function `h = (1,2,3,4,5,4,3,2,1)` (series
`(1-t^5)^2/(1-t)^2`). Mixed block, split by `Y2`-parity (the "character" used in the chapter):

    h_even = (1,1,2,2,3,2,2,1,1),     h_odd = (0,1,1,2,2,2,1,1,0)

(monomial parity counts minus the two quintic generators, of opposite `Y2`-parity, times degrees
0..3; the first syzygy sits in degree 10). The degree-12 globally odd quotient has dimension
**866** = `Σ_{d0+d1+d2=12} h(d0) h(d1) h_parity(d2)`, parity even iff `d1` odd. Allowed degree-12
`Y`-odd monomials: **3080**; Jacobian part **2214**.

### 7.2 Raising by coefficient directions (imported from Chapter 2, "Coefficient raising")

If `F_t = F + tT` and `v_q(t)` is a strict family (`q v_q(t)(F_t) = A`, `div v_q(t) = 0`, `A`
fixed), with dot = `d/dt` at 0:

    v_{q+1} = ( q T v_q - F v̇_q )/(q+1) - v_q(T) E_6 / [6(q+1)]      serves A·T at pole order q+1,
    N_{q+1} = [ q (T D + F Ḋ) N_q - F D Ṅ_q - (D/6) N_q(T) E_6 ] / (q+1)     if v_q = N_q/D^q.

"Coefficient differentiation raises the power of `D` by at most one and introduces no new physical
divisor." Used here with `T` any monomial of `P0` or `P1`, or any of the four even monomials of the
mixed block (`X2^6, X2^4Y2^2, X2^2Y2^4, Y2^6`). Existence of the `t`-derivative at every smooth
member: contact presentations form a finite étale cover (formal lifts), chosen spanning minors stay
nonzero, and the two-reflection fields lift because the 27 lines form a finite étale cover over the

### 7.3 Profile exhaustion at degree 12 (quotient part)

* `d0 ≥ 6` or `d1 ≥ 6`: factor a sextic coefficient direction `T` from that block; raise the
  `q=1` field of the remaining degree-6 target.
* `d2 ≥ 7`: the four even mixed sextic monomials times degree 1 or 2 give every degree-7/8 mixed
  monomial; raise.
* `d2 = 6` and `d1` odd: the mixed character must be even, so the mixed factor is itself a
  coefficient direction; raise.
* any `d_i = 4`: block Euler (`D = P_i`).
* remaining (each `d_i ≤ 8`): exactly `(5,5,2), (5,2,5), (2,5,5)`, globally odd dimensions
  **32, 24, 24** [verified here]. Served by the `q=2` first-integral product fields of §2.3 with
  `β = C_i C_j Q_k`, `Q_k` running over all quadratics of the third block; the `C·J` moment theorem
  spans the degree-5 quotient in each active block.

### 7.4 Jacobian ideal at `q = 2` (reusable lift)

If `A = 2 w(F)` for a parity-correct polynomial field `w`, put `B = div w` (degree 6, `Y`-odd) and
let `u` be any `q=1` field for `B`. Then

    K_2(F, A; w + F u) = F · K_1(F, B; u) = 0        [verified here]

so `w + Fu` serves `A` at `q=2` with the denominator of `u`; Euler normalization then gives a strict
divergence-free field. Totals: 866 (quotient) + 2214 (Jacobian) = 3080.

## 10. Proven facts vs. evidence vs. assertions vs. gaps (skeptical reading)

Proven in the chapter with complete arguments: H3 smoothness criterion; all §2a.1 identities;
the 40-sheet cover and its étaleness; the raw moment theorem (modulo the chart-independence
sentence); the quintic moment theorem (residue duality + the `4Θ` divisor + the torsion bound);

Verified independently here (§0): every displayed identity I could turn into a polynomial check.

Imported from Chapter 2 and used as black boxes: subset Euler; the two-reflection cubic-surface
theorem (27 lines, degree-45 tritangent denominator, dominance/legality argument, partial-target
scope); the raising identity; existence of formal lifts for the two-reflection fields.

Asserted / thin spots a formalizer would have to fill:
* "Projective changes of the plane and linear changes of the projection pencil preserve the span
  rank" (reduction of every squarefree `P` to the Hesse chart, including centers with third
  coordinate zero).
* "Linear target separation on the finite covers supplies the required combinations": the
  coefficients live in the compositum of several sheet fields; the chapter does not fix which
  sheets, nor bound the field degree.
* `q=2` lifting: "for each target choose spanning columns with a nonzero minor at the point; their
  inverse coefficient matrix and the polynomial flux formulas have regular formal coefficient lifts"
  — correct in principle (étale cover), but the actual derivative `v̇_1` must be computed per target.
* Parity averaging over the Klein group `⟨X2 ↦ -X2, Y2 ↦ -Y2⟩` is invoked for presentations of the
  mixed block (which need not respect those parities); legal and degree-preserving but doubles the
  denominator.
* Nothing is said about `q ≥ 3` for this family (Chapter 4 table: "1,2").

Not claimed (and should not be inferred): any denominator-degree bound; any statement for sextics
without the three-block split; any statement for blocks that mix `X` and `Y` coordinates with
non-even sextics (such a block would break global `Y`-evenness anyway).
