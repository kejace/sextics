## 3. The most promising mechanism, and exactly where it fails
**Candidate: the weighted complete-intersection model X ≅ W ∩ {u = L²} with W Fano (fact 5c), i.e. X as a degree-2 divisor in a reflection-quotient Fano fivefold.** All of H⁵(W) is divisor-supported (§3.2), and H⁴_prim(X)(−1) = coker(H⁵(W) → H⁵(W∖X)) by the residue sequence. Transfer of support requires a dominant generically finite e: A¹×S → W∖X (then the §3.2 trace argument gives η|_{W∖(X∪E)} = 0 and ξ = Res η supported on E∩X). This needs covering rational curves of W meeting X in ONE point. Weighted lines of W (a 4-dimensional family, expected) meet the degree-2 divisor X in two points; the correspondence Z = {(s,p): p ∈ C_s ∩ X} then gives only m·ξ + T ξ ∈ N¹ with T the "other intersection point" self-correspondence, which is tautological (T ≡ −m mod N¹ whenever H⁴(S) ⊂ N¹). A single intersection point would need κ̄(W∖X) = −∞, but K_W + X = O(0), so κ̄ = 0: **the complement is not A¹-uniruled and the route is closed**; more generally, for a CY divisor X ⊂ W one has (K_W+X)|_X = 0, and κ̄(W∖X) = −∞ forces an isotrivial product-like situation whose residue image is tau-anti-invariant. The same obstruction kills the pencil of Fano fivefolds Y_λ = Φ⁶ ∩ H_λ ⊃ X (sextics in P⁶, K = O(−1); X = Y_λ ∩ H has K_Y + X = O(0)).

So within the book's toolkit no mechanism applies; what the eight-power structure DOES supply are explicit geometric denominator families ("vertical" divisors: pullbacks of curves on the (eps_X,eps_Y,t) base, ring C[e(X),e(Y),L,M]; pullbacks of surfaces on the (x,t)- or (y,t)-bases, rings C[X0,X1,X2,M], C[L,Y0,Y1,Y2]) whose E1 kernels are being computed (job `construct-spp-vert`), with D_res the distinguished degree-10 member.
