## 0. What the chapter delivers and what it does not

* It proves *existence of divisor support* (hence, by Chapter 1's prescribed-support theorem,
  existence of a certificate with denominator `D_red^q` or `D^q`) for residue classes that are served
  by a finite quotient whose smooth model is (a) covered by rational curves (uniruled, in practice
  Fano) or (b) fibred over a surface by surfaces with `p_g = q = 0`.
* Denominators are **abstract** everywhere except where the chapter cites the explicit
  polynomial-family integrations of §2.7 (PR #150 weighted quartic, PR #153 cubic-cover line). The
  geometric theorems produce `D` only as "a homogeneous equation containing a computed bad locus"
  (graph boundary images, positive-dimensional fibres, branch locus, exceptional images), with **no
  degree bound** ("The proof bounds poles on a chosen support; it supplies no bound on the degree
  `e` of the support itself" is Chapter 1's phrasing; §3.5 adds "This is a bound for curves in a
  fixed embedding, not a bound for denominator degree").
* Every negative statement is a *representation/character* boundary of a specific mechanism. The
  chapter never claims nonexistence of a certificate for any target.
* Lean counterparts (from `formal/README.md`): `HodgeComputer.Constructions.AveragingProjectors`
  (§3.1 ideal argument); `HodgeComputer.Constructions.PolynomialCurves` (arithmetic lifting of a
  nonsingular square incidence to a finite extension of the generic coefficient field, and
  conversion of a dominant polynomial-curve family with polynomial normal into normalized certificate
  data — the engine behind the §3.3/§3.4 explicit realizations); `HoldoutCover` (§2.7 cover and
  moving conic).

## 1. §3.1 Divisor support and its source

### 1.1 Gysin description of `N^1 H^k(X)` [PROVEN, inputs: localization, purity, strictness of MHS morphisms]

* Definition: a class is divisor-supported if it vanishes on `X \ Z` for a divisor `Z`; the subspace
  is `N^1 H^k(X)`.
* Take an embedded resolution `r: X~ -> X` with reduced inverse image `E = U E_i` SNC. Mayer–Vietoris
  for supports + purity: the `c`-fold intersections contribute `H^{k-c-1}(E_{i1} ∩ ... ∩ E_{ic})(-c)`
  of weight `k + c - 1`; only `c = 1` has weight `k`. Strictness ⇒ image in pure `H^k(X~)` equals
  the image of `sum_i H^{k-2}(E_i)(-1)`; push forward with `r_* r^* = id`.
* **Result:** `N^1 H^k(X)` is generated by proper Gysin images from smooth divisors (resolutions of
  the actual components).

### 1.2 Consequence on fourfolds and the sextic-specific singularity constraint [PROVEN]

* The `(3,1)` part of a Gysin image comes from `H^{2,0}(E_i)`. Components contracted into
  codimension ≥ 2 contribute only type `(2,2)` (degree-zero classes of surfaces with twist `(-2)`).
  "A count of singularities or contact multiplicities does not determine their Gysin map."
* On a smooth sextic fourfold: `Pic(X) = Z[O_X(1)]` ⇒ every nonzero effective `D` is ample;
  `0 -> O_X(-D) -> O_X -> O_D -> 0`, `H^2(X,O_X) = 0`, Kodaira + Serre duality ⇒ `H^2(D,O_D) = 0`.
  If `D` is normal with rational singularities, `H^{2,0}(D~) = 0` ⇒ zero `(3,1)` Gysin image.
* **Hard constraint on denominators (q = 1 targets, and the (3,1) part of q = 2 targets):** any
  support of a nonzero `(3,1)` class contains a component which is **non-normal or has non-rational
  singularities**. Replacing a support by a smooth divisor need not preserve its cohomological role.
  Practical reading: a candidate `V(D) ∩ X` that is smooth or has only rational singularities cannot
  serve a nonzero invariant `q = 1` target, whatever the numerator search.

### 1.3 Functoriality [PROVEN]

* Finite sums: union of supports.
* Dominant generically finite proper `p` between smooth varieties of equal dimension: `p^*` and
  `p_*` preserve `N^1`; `p_* p^* = deg(p) id` (projection formula).
* Finite group `G` acting on `M`: `H^*(M/G, Q) = H^*(M, Q)^G`; with `pi` the quotient map
  `pi_* pi^* = |G| id`, `pi^* pi_* = sum_{g in G} g^*`; valid for branched quotients (the
  top-dimensional cycle of `M x_{M/G} M` is the sum of the graphs of the group elements).

### 1.4 Quotient-transfer lemma [PROVEN]

`M` smooth projective with faithful finite `G`-action, `N` a smooth projective model of `M/G`. Then
`H^k(N) ⊂ N^1` iff `H^k(M)^G ⊂ N^1`.

Proof ingredients (reusable): equivariant resolution `r: R -> M` (blowups with smooth centres),
`p: R -> N` generically finite of degree `|G|`; blowup formula
`H^k(Bl_C M) = H^k(M) ⊕ ⊕_{j=1}^{codim C - 1} H^{k-2j}(C)(-j)` (summands `gamma -> i_*(xi^{j-1} pi^* gamma)`,
`xi = c_1(O_E(1))`, `E = P(N_{C/M})`), so `ker r_*` is supported on exceptional divisors. Forward:
`r_* p^* beta` supported ⇒ `r^* r_* p^* beta` supported; `p^* beta` differs by an exceptional class;
push forward by `p`, divide by `|G|`. Converse: `p^* p_* r^* alpha = sum_g g^* r^* alpha = |G| r^* alpha`
on the dense open; the difference is supported on the omitted boundary ("explains why the boundary
cannot be dropped"). Same proof: the all-supported assertion is invariant under smooth projective
birational modification. "These constructions use algebraic graphs, blowups and traces. An abstract
Hodge isomorphism alone would not transport a supporting equation."

### 1.5 Averaging projectors: the exact scope of a family of quotients [PROVEN]

* `e_K = (1/|K|) sum_{k in K} k in Q[G]`; left action `g -> (g^{-1})^*` (image of averaging is
  convention-independent).
* If smooth projective models of `M/K_1, ..., M/K_s` have divisor-supported `H^m`, then with
  `I = sum_j Q[G] e_{K_j} Q[G]` (two-sided ideal) one has `I · H^m(M) ⊆ N^1 H^m(M)`.
* Maschke: a simple rational representation `rho` occurs in `I` iff `rho^{K_j} ≠ 0` for some `j`.
  Hence the supported invariant spaces plus their `G`-translates serve the entire isotypic component
  of every such `rho`; "Any projector lying in `I` has supported image."
* [BOUNDARY] "This is a criterion for these quotients, not an assertion that the remaining
  representations lack support from other correspondences." "Finding more curves on a fixed quotient
  improves its construction, but cannot add a representation absent from its averaging projector."
* Auxiliary covers: resolve a projective model equivariantly, compute the ideal there, use the proper
  generically finite map down; a physical class is served when its pullback lies in the supported
  image; normalized pushforward descends the support. "Reflections, deck traces and exchanges are
  instances of this one procedure."

## 2. §3.2 Curve and surface fibrations

### 2.1 Support theorem (covering rational curves) [PROVEN]

Hypotheses: `W` smooth projective `n`-fold, `n > 0`; an actual morphism `e: P^1 x S -> W`, dominant
and generically finite; `S` smooth integral affine of dimension `n - 1`.
Conclusion: all of `H^n(W)` is supported on one divisor, obtainable by finite algebraic operations
from `e`.

Proof/algorithm (this *is* the denominator recipe):
1. `V = A^1 x S`; `H^n_dR(V) = H^n_dR(S) = 0` (affine de Rham complex ends in degree `n - 1`).
2. `Gamma` = closure of the graph of `e` in a projective compactification; `p: Gamma -> W` proper,
   generically finite; `dim(Gamma \ V) ≤ n - 1`, so its image is a proper closed subset.
3. Remove that image, the positive-dimensional-fibre locus of `p`, and the non-étale locus
   (characteristic zero); enlarge the union to a divisor `E`.
4. `p^{-1}(W \ E) ⊂ V` is finite étale of degree `m > 0` over `W \ E`; pullback of `alpha` vanishes
   there; **trace: `m · alpha|_{W \ E} = 0`.** (No vanishing of all of `H^n(p^{-1}(W \ E))` is needed.)
5. Elimination computes boundary images, fibre-dimension and branch loci; any nonzero homogeneous
   equation containing their union is `D_W`. Pull back to `X` through the quotient map.

Unparametrized families: split the generic conic by a finite extension and shrink the base. §2.7's
function-field construction is the residue-primitive-level analogue (polynomial curve family,
invertible normal matrix, polynomial integration, finite trace) and "keeps track of the source
density, which is necessary when transferring only a selected cohomological sector."

### 2.2 Bounded Fano input [EXT: Debarre Thm 7.5, 7.7, Prop 9.12, Cor 9.13; Miyaoka–Mori]

* Smooth Fano `n`-fold: covered by rational curves `C` with `-K · C ≤ n + 1`.
* Normal projective `n`-fold with ample Q-Cartier `-K`: covered by rational curves with
  `-K · C ≤ 2n` (from Miyaoka–Mori `H · C ≤ 2n (H · B)/(-K · B)`, `B` a general complete-intersection
  curve in the smooth locus, `H` a Cartier multiple of `-K`).
* Characteristic zero: a general member of a covering family on a smooth variety is free.
* Incidence scheme: embed by a very ample `L`, translate the bound into `L`-degree; for each allowed
  `e` substitute binary `e`-forms into the equations of `W` plus the basepoint-free condition;
  finitely many components; one has dominant marked evaluation (test by elimination or differential
  rank).
* Slicing to the theorem's input: at a point where the curve is nonconstant and marked evaluation is
  submersive, choose `n - 1` parameter tangent directions which with the curve direction span `T_W`;
  an affine linear slice realizes them; shrink to affine `S` of dimension `n - 1`.
* Singular `W`: resolve first; the generic parametrized curve is not in the exceptional image, lifts
  by properness; "Curves passing through singular points downstairs must remain in the bounded
  search" (do not impose avoidance or a weighted-coordinate lift).
* [BOUNDARY] "It asserts no practical runtime bound." The degree bound is for the search downstairs,
  not for lifted curves or the eventual support.

### 2.3 Surface-fibration theorem [PROVEN; inputs: Lefschetz (1,1), Hodge index, finiteness of integral isometries of a definite lattice, relative Picard/Brauer splitting]

Hypotheses: `W` smooth projective fourfold with a dominant rational map to a surface; geometric
generic fibre geometrically connected with smooth projective model `T`, `p_g(T) = q(T) = 0`
(fibre need not be rational or uniruled).
Conclusion: `H^4(W, Q) = N^1 H^4(W, Q)`.

Proof skeleton (reusable identities):
* Resolve, shrink base to a smooth affine surface `B`, smooth projective `f: V -> B`.
* Fibres: `H^1 = H^3 = 0`, all of `H^2` is `(1,1)` hence algebraic. Monodromy preserves the relative
  ample class; Hodge index ⇒ its orthogonal complement is negative definite ⇒ finite monodromy ⇒
  `R^2 f_* Q` constant after a finite étale base change.
* Divisor classes `d_1..d_b` realized by line bundles after a further finite extension (relative
  `Pic^0` trivial since `q = 0`; Brauer obstruction split by a finite extension); `e` = square of the
  relative ample class.
* Leray–Hirsch: `R f_* Q ≅ Q ⊕ Q(-1)[-2]^b ⊕ Q(-2)[-4]`, so
  `H^4(V) = H^4(B) ⊕ sum_i d_i ∪ f^* H^2(B) ⊕ e · H^0(B)` with `H^4(B) = 0` (affine surface).
* Every term vanishes off the chosen divisors (incl. one representing the ample class); closures,
  finite étale trace, deleted loci and boundaries all go into one divisor.
* [NOT CLAIMED] Bloch's conjecture or Chow-motive decompositions. [BOUNDARY] "The proof establishes
  existence of divisor support; explicit equations for those divisor families are additional data
  needed for a computed certificate."
* Applied in Chapter 4 (§4.2) to paired-sign sextics (`X_i, Y_i -> -X_i, -Y_i` simultaneously for
  each `i`, plus an extra order-three diagonal symmetry). §4.7 quantifies why the hypothesis is
  special: the surface over passive parameters `c_i = (Y_i/X_i)/(Y_0/X_0)`, `b = (Y_0/X_0)^2` is
  bidegree `(6,3)` in `P^2 x P^1` with `K = O(3,1)`, `q = 0`, `p_g = 20` for a general even sextic;
  paired signs give `(3,3)` with `p_g = 2`; the order-three quotient gives `p_g = 0`
  (sequence `20 -> 2 -> 0`). On the `(3,3)` surface the nontrivial order-three characters have Hodge
  numbers `(1,10,1)` each, so the theorem "does not extend to a nontrivial character merely by
  replacing the numerator or increasing its pole order."

## 3. §3.3 Reflection quotients

### 3.1 Reflection theorem [PROVEN modulo 2.2]

* Hypothesis on `F`: smooth sextic **even in one coordinate `z`**: `F(z, x) = H(z^2, x_1..x_5)`
  (commutes with block-linear coordinate changes, so "even in a linear form inside a block" is the
  real hypothesis). The `z^6` coefficient is automatically nonzero (otherwise the `z`-axis point lies
  on `X` with all partials zero, since evenness forbids `z^5 x_i`).
* Targets served: every `A` **odd in `z`**, every `q ≥ 1`; retain the global `Y`-odd requirement.
* Quotient: `W = {H(u, x) = 0} ⊂ P(2,1,1,1,1,1)`, `u = z^2`. `W` misses the unique weighted singular
  point and is smooth; weighted adjunction `-K_W = O_W(1)` ample (equivalently Hurwitz:
  `pi^*(-K_W) = O_X(1)`, `K_X = 0`, branch divisor class `O_X(1)`).
* Residue transfer: `Omega` changes sign under `sigma: z -> -z`; odd `A` ⇒ `sigma`-invariant residue
  `alpha_A = pi^* beta_A`, `beta_A = pi_* alpha_A / 2`. Covering rational curves on `W` compute a
  divisor supporting `H^4(W)`; pull it back to `X`.
* Denominator: pullback of the §2.1 bad-locus equation under `u = z^2`; degree unbounded; not
  explicit in the chapter.

### 3.2 Incidence numbers for the reflection quotient [COMP, arithmetic re-checked]

* A weighted degree-`e` map `P^1 -> P(2,1^5)`: five binary `e`-forms and one binary `2e`-form,
  `7e + 6` coefficients; `H(phi) = 0` is a binary form of degree `6e`: `6e + 1` equations.
* `e ≤ 5` suffices (smooth Fano bound `-K_W · C ≤ 5` with `-K_W = O(1)`). The five weight-one forms
  must have no common zero. Largest incidence: **41 coefficients, 31 equations** (`e = 5`).
* Freeness is an exact rank condition: `H^1(phi^* T_W(-1)) = 0` ⟺ surjectivity of
  `H^0(O(e-1)^5 ⊕ O(2e-1)) -> H^0(O(6e-1))`, the map being multiplication by the six partials of
  `H` at `phi`; matrix size `6e x 7e`. Splitting theorem: this is nonnegativity of the splitting
  degrees of `phi^* T_W`.
* At a free point the untwisted incidence derivative has rank `6e + 1`, so the incidence is smooth
  of dimension `e + 5`; choose `e + 2` affine slices (with the evaluation rank condition) to leave a
  three-dimensional `S`. "These slices choose a covering family on the given `W`; they restrict
  neither `F` nor `A`."

### 3.3 Three reflections ⇒ all targets [PROVEN]

If `F` is separately even in `Y_0, Y_1, Y_2` (or separately in `X_0, X_1, X_2`): decompose odd `A`
into simultaneous characters of `(Z/2)^3` (per-coordinate parities); each `Y`-odd character has at
least one odd `Y_i`, so it is served by that quotient; add the fields (Chapter 1 addition formula
`D_{A+B} = DE`, `N_{A+B} = EN + DM`, `Q_{A+B} = E^2 Q + D^2 R`) and take the union of pulled-back
supports. The `X` version follows because `deg A = 6q` is even, so `Y`-odd ⇒ `X`-odd. All-`q`
numerator extraction is §3.6.

### 3.4 Algebraic realization by weighted quartics (PR #150 = `reflection-coupled-fano`) [COMP, verified here]

* `F` (coupling `5/7`, even only in `Y_2`): `X0^6 + 5/7 X0^3 X1 Y0 Y1 + 5/7 X0 X1 Y0^3 Y1 + 5/7 X0 X1 Y0^2 Y2^2 + 10/7 X0 X1 Y1^2 Y2^2 + 15/7 X0 X2^3 Y0 Y1 + X1^6 + 10/7 X1^3 X2 Y0 Y1 + 5/7 X1 X2 Y0 Y1^3 + X2^6 + Y0^6 + Y1^6 + Y2^6`; target `A = X0^3 Y2^3`.
* Reduction mod 5 of the quotient equation: `H_0 = x_0^6 + x_1^6 + x_2^6 + x_3^6 + x_4^6 + u^3`
  (over `F_25`, `j^2 = 2`).
* **Seed curve (weighted quartic, weights (1,1,1,1,1,2)):**
  ```
  x0 = j (s^4 + t^4)          x1 = (2 + j)(s^3 t + s t^3)     x2 = j s^2 t^2
  x3 = j (s^3 t - s t^3)      x4 = s^4 - t^4                  u  = s^8 + s^4 t^4 + t^8
  ```
  It lies on `H_0 = 0` and the five weight-one coordinates have no common zero
  (`x2` vanishes only at `s = 0` or `t = 0`, where `x0 = j t^4` resp. `j s^4`).
* Incidence: chart `s = 1`; vary the coefficients of `t, ..., t^4` in all six coordinates (24
  unknowns), keep the `t^8` coefficient of `u` equal to 1; the 24 positive coefficients `t^1..t^24` of
  `H_0(phi)` give an invertible `24 x 24` matrix. Also inverting the nonzero linear coefficient of
  `x_1` preserves transversality to the endpoint section. The constant coefficient is the endpoint
  relation.
* `fqlx`'s construction: lift the square incidence over the integral endpoint ring with `5/7` fixed;
  independent endpoint constants + the transverse coordinate prove dominance; finite type and
  transcendence degree give a finite extension of the quotient function field; the inverse incidence
  matrix supplies a polynomial normal; integration on that family, finite trace and legal clearing
  give normalized quotient certificate data (§2.7); pullback through `u = Y_2^2` gives the
  reflection-odd target. The accepted `Solution.lean` is existential (`exists_generic_field`,
  `physical_certificate_of_quartic_family`): **no explicit `D` is recorded**.
* [BOUNDARY] "Even a complete primitive construction on the quotient reaches the density sector of
  its pullback. A target even in `Y_2` has the opposite residue character and does not follow by
  changing only the quotient source."

### 3.5 Finite groups generated by reflections [PROVEN]

Hypotheses: finite linear `G` preserving `F`, generated by order-two hyperplane reflections, with
`tau in G` or `-tau in G`. Conclusion: **all targets supported** (all `q`).
Argument: `U = sum_s H^4(X)^s` (sum over reflections `s`) is supported and `G`-stable; on
`H^4(X)/U` each reflection acts by `-1` (`v + s^* v in H^4(X)^s`), so `G` acts through `det`;
`det(tau) = det(-tau) = -1` in six variables and both induce the same projective involution; their
invariants therefore lie in `U`. Mere normalization of `G` by `tau` is insufficient; no commutativity
needed.

Global involution alone: fixed locus = two plane sextic curves `X ∩ {X = 0}`, `X ∩ {Y = 0}`
(codimension 3), transverse singularities `1/2(1,1,1)`; "It supplies no branch divisor or Fano
quotient"; nonuniruledness of `X/tau` (Kodaira dimension 0, `K` torsion, Kollár–Larsen) and the
obstruction for its natural cubic cover are in §4.7.

### 3.6 Finite quotients beyond reflections: the age criterion [EXT: Kollár–Larsen Thm 2, Cor 4]

* `g` finite-order automorphism of a smooth projective Calabi–Yau; at a fixed point write tangent
  eigenvalues `exp(2 pi i a_j)`, `0 ≤ a_j < 1`; **age** `= sum a_j`. If some fixed point has
  `0 < age < 1`, the quotient by `g` is uniruled ⇒ a resolution is covered by rational curves ⇒
  slicing gives the §2.1 input ⇒ `g`-invariant middle cohomology is divisor-supported.
  For a smooth sextic fourfold (`K` trivial, simply connected, `b_2 = 1`) Cor 4 even gives rational
  connectedness; only uniruledness is used. Existence only: no explicit curves, no degree bound.
* **Residue character formula** (diagonal action fixing `F` exactly, weights `w_j mod n`): a monomial
  `Z^e` has residue character `sum_j (e_j + 1) w_j (mod n)`. The `+1` is the volume form.
  Invariance of the polynomial numerator alone is not the test. The §1.5 ideal combines the supported
  residue spaces and their translates. "The cover construction in §2.7 can reach further characters
  because its useful reflection acts on an auxiliary variety rather than on the original fourfold."

## 4. §3.4 Nonlinear polynomial Piola maps are finite Fano maps

### 4.1 Theorem [PROVEN modulo 2.2 and standard stack/weighted facts]

Hypotheses: `Phi = (Phi_1..Phi_6)` homogeneous of degrees `m_i`, `M = sum m_i`, `delta = det(d Phi)`;
`1 ≤ m_i ≤ 5`, `7 ≤ M ≤ 12`, `F = G(Phi)` with `wt(G) = 6`, `X = {F = 0}` smooth.
**No symmetry of `F` is required.**
Conclusions:
* `Phi` is finite; `W = {G = 0} ⊂ P(m_1..m_6)` is a normal Fano fourfold with quotient singularities.
* For every `q ≥ 1` and weighted polynomial `b` of weight `6(q+1) - M`, the target
  `A = delta · b(Phi)` is divisor-supported with a finite geometric certificate construction.
  Retain the odd parity in the involution problem. No spectral derivation or eigencondition on `b`.

Proof pieces:
* Finiteness: no variable has weight 6, so `G` has no constant or linear term; a common zero `z ≠ 0`
  of the `Phi_i` would give `F(z) = 0`, `grad F(z) = d Phi(z)^T grad G(0) = 0`; so the `Phi_i` are a
  homogeneous system of parameters, `Phi` finite surjective, algebraically independent, `delta ≠ 0`.
* Quasismoothness: a critical zero `y ≠ 0` of `G` lifts to a singular point of `F`.
* Well-formedness (Sano–Tasin §1.1) because degree 6 exceeds every weight, **except** the profiles
  `(1,2,2,2,2,2)` and `(2,2,2,2,2,2)`: there every monomial of `G` is even in the weight-one variable
  `t`; with `u = t^2` (second Veronese / regrading) `W` is an ordinary **smooth cubic in `P^5`**;
  coarse anticanonical class = 6 × old rational hyperplane class ≥ `M - 6`.
* Weighted adjunction (Dolgachev): `-K_W = O_W(M - 6)`, ample, Q-Cartier.
* **Piola pullback identity**: with `Omega_z = iota_{eps_z} dz`, `Omega_m = iota_{sum m_i t_i d/dt_i} dt`,
  homogeneity gives `d Phi(eps_z) = eps_m ∘ Phi`, hence
  `Phi^*(Omega_m) = delta · Omega_z` and `Phi^*(b Omega_m / G^(q+1)) = delta · b(Phi) · Omega_z / F^(q+1)`.
  The downstairs form is weight zero and horizontal, a global closed form on the complement of
  `G = 0` in the smooth weighted stack; residue gives a global `beta in H^4(W)` (stack cohomology =
  coarse cohomology rationally) with `alpha_A = pi^* beta`. [NOT CLAIMED] that arbitrary classes on
  `W_reg` extend across singularities.
* Pull `beta` to a resolution `W~`, support all `H^4(W~)` by 2.2, remove from `W` the images of that
  support and of the exceptional locus; `pi^{-1}` of these are proper closed subsets of `X`, enlarge
  to a divisor. Denominator: abstract, unbounded degree.

### 4.2 Why every primitive nonlinear polynomial atom is included [PROVEN]

* `q = 1`, `A = b(Phi) delta` of degree 6 ⇒ `wt(b) = 12 - M`, so `6 ≤ M ≤ 12`; nonlinear ⇒ `M > 6`.
* A weight ≥ 7 forces `(7,1,1,1,1,1)`; then `G` ignores that coordinate and `F` depends on five
  linear forms — contradicts smoothness.
* A weight 6: write `G = c t_j + H`; `c ≠ 0` (else five forms of weight ≤ 2 share a nonzero zero,
  singular for `F`). Then the **adjugate lift**
  `B = b(Phi) · adj(d Phi) · e_j / c` has degree 1 and `B(F) = A`
  (chain rule `grad F = d Phi^T grad G(Phi)`, `d Phi · adj(d Phi) = delta I`, `dG/dt_j = c`), so
  `A in J(F)_6`; after parity averaging `div B` is a `Y`-odd constant, hence 0: zero primitive target.
* Hence every nonzero primitive nonlinear polynomial atom satisfies the theorem's hypotheses.
  [BOUNDARY] "Linear maps and arbitrary rational adapted maps are not covered by this argument."
* Example: six arbitrary globally `Y`-even quadrics composed with any cubic `G` (profile

## 6. §3.6 From a computed support to every `q` [PROVEN]

* Enlarge a computed proper support in `X` to a hypersurface `D = 0` with `F ∤ D`. If finite
  coefficient extensions were used, take the union of conjugates; also take the union with the global
  involution image. The product of equations is a **rational, globally `Y`-even** support equation.
* `1 ≤ q ≤ 4`: Chapter 1 supplies a numerator over `delta = D^q`; Euler normalization makes extraction
  the complete linear system
  `q N(F) = A delta`, `delta div N - N(delta) = F^q R`, `deg N_i = q (deg D + 6) - 5`,
  over all coefficients of the prescribed degrees and parities. Consistency is guaranteed by the
  support theorem; exact linear algebra finds `N`. "This is a fixed-denominator solve, with no guessed
  degree search remaining."
* `q ≥ 5`: the six quintic partials form a regular sequence with Jacobian-ring socle degree 24, so
  `A = sum_i B_i F_i` with `deg B_i = 6q - 5`. Average `B` to impose parity, put `c = div B`
  (degree `6(q-1)`, `Y`-odd). If `w` serves `(q-1, c)` then
  `v = (B + F w)/q` serves `(q, A)`, with `K_q(A; v) = (F/q) · K_{q-1}(c; w)`.
  (Re-derived: `q v(F) = A + F w(F)`, `F div v = (F/q)(c + w(F) + F div w)`, so
  `K_q = (F/q)(c - (q-1) w(F) + F div w)`.) Inducts to `q ≤ 4`, adds no support.
* [BOUNDARY] "The theorem supplies a finite procedure for legal certificates; it does not promise
  strictness, a small denominator, or a practical runtime."
