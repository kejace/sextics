## 4. What remains unexplored (concrete gaps)

Nothing beyond one character computation has been done publicly. The gaps, ordered by how directly they bear on existence:

1. **No denominator of degree ≥ 7 has ever been tested** (and degree ≤ 6 only for 11 structural shapes + one cubic cone). The colon space (J:A)/J is 20-dimensional at d=6 and 63-dimensional at d=7 and its bases are saved, but **no element of these spaces has been run through the full fixed-D system** (only H and B, which happen to be in it, were run — both inconsistent). A systematic solve over the d=6 colon space (20 parameters — but the full system is linear in N,Q only for *fixed* D, so one must either enumerate special members or run a nonlinear joint search in (D,N,Q)) has not been attempted.
2. **No symmetry-adapted search.** The fixed-D system can be decomposed by the Z/6 × Z/2 × Z/2 action: if D is G-semi-invariant, N and Q decompose into G-characters, cutting unknowns and equations by ≈ 24 (≈ 6 at least via the h-grading, since every monomial has a well-defined (X2,Y2)-degree mod 6). Never implemented for this instance.
3. **The 4+2 Thom–Sebastiani reduction has not been used algebraically.** For a denominator D that depends only on the core variables, or only on (X2,Y2), or is a sum D_H(X0,X1,Y0,Y1) + D_B(X2,Y2), the identity E = F²Q splits by (X2,Y2)-weight; this bigrading reduces the linear algebra enormously and may force structural conclusions (e.g. for D = D_H(core) the (X2,Y2)-weight-0 piece is a *four-variable* certificate problem for (H, A) on the sextic surface S_H — a (2,0)-type class on a surface, where the §3.1 obstruction says a divisor supporting a (2,0) class must be non-normal / have non-rational singularities — never written down).
4. **The core side has not been studied at all.** Everything in the thread is about C_B. The core target A·U ∈ H^{2,1}(T_H)_ζ (68-dim) on the CY threefold T_H: u³ = H ⊂ P(1,1,1,1,2); T_H's own Hodge structure may well be *special* (H has only 10 monomials with coefficients 5/7, 10/7, 15/7 — a very sparse "chain" surface); e.g. does S_H ⊂ P^3 (the sextic surface) or T_H carry extra automorphisms or a fibration making H^{2,1}(T_H)_ζ (partly) algebraic-supported? Nobody checked.
5. **The Fano fourfold companion W_H = {H + s³ + t³ = 0} ⊂ P(1,1,1,1,2,2)** (−K = O(2), the 4-variable analogue of 3A.2) is Fano and gives, via Chapter 3, divisor support for all of H^4(W_H)... wait, W_H is a 4-fold so the relevant group is H^4(W_H) ⊇ [H^3(T_H) ⊗ H^1(E_Fermat)]^{μ3}. This supports H^{2,1}(T_H)_ζ ⊗ H^{1,0}(E)_{ζ²} — i.e. the **core factor of our target paired with the Fermat elliptic curve**. The missing step is to replace the Fermat elliptic factor by the simple Prym P (or by C_B itself). In 3A.3 the replacement is done by a translated diagonal on E × E; here the two curves differ (E_Fermat vs C_B), so one needs a correspondence E_Fermat ⇝ C_B sending H^{1,0}(E)_{ζ²} to ⟨dt/w⟩ — impossible as a Hodge correspondence since P is simple of dimension 3 and E is an elliptic curve (any correspondence E → P factors through an elliptic subvariety). So the obstruction is sharp: **the needed cohomological piece is H^{2,1}(T_H)_ζ ⊗ H^{1,0}(P)_{ζ²}, with P simple**. A support construction has to come from a variety whose cohomology contains H^1(P) — e.g. a Fano/uniruled or surface-fibered variety dominating (T_H × C_B)/μ3 or a correspondence from a K3-type surface (W_B is K3) — none proposed.
6. **The K3 companion W_B = {B + s³ + t³ = 0} ⊂ P(1,1,2,2)**: its transcendental lattice vs. H^1(C_B)⊗H^1(E_Fermat) is the natural link between the Prym and a K3 (a Kuga–Satake-like relation in miniature). Nobody has computed the Picard rank of W_B or checked whether a Shioda–Inose / Kummer-type structure of W_B makes H^1(P) ⊗ H^1(E) algebraic-correspondent to K3 cohomology. (The §3A.6 Kuga–Satake criterion applies to K3 surfaces with non-symplectic order-3 automorphisms whose fixed locus contains a curve — W_B *does* have the order-3 automorphism (s,t) → (ζs, ζt)… wait that scales both s and t, which acts on the holomorphic form; its fixed locus on W_B is {s=t=0}∩W_B = {B=0} = 6 points, not a curve; and s → ζs alone is not a symmetry of s³+t³? It is: (s,t) → (ζs, t) preserves s³. Its fixed locus is {s=0} ∩ W_B = {B + t³ = 0} ⊂ P(1,1,2) — the curve C_B itself (genus 4)! So W_B carries a non-symplectic order-3 automorphism with a fixed curve, and Bolognesi–Laterveer gives it an **algebraic Kuga–Satake correspondence** — whether that is usable for the (2,1)⊗(1,0) piece is an open question nobody has raised.)
7. **Deformation/continuation from solved neighbours**: never tried. Natural one-parameter families: (a) turn off the coupling K → F_0 = f + g + B = three decoupled binary blocks (served by the §2a theorem for all targets, explicit strict certificates with d=4-ish denominators); continue the certificate in the coupling parameter c (K → c·K, c from 0 to 1) — the §2a denominators r_i² + P_j are built from the contact presentations P = C³ + r² of the binary sextics and would have to be deformed; (b) deform B to a Fermat-like or to a block making the Prym split (the signature-(2,1) family is 2-dimensional in moduli; the locus where P ~ E'×A is a countable union of divisors; at such a member the elliptic contraction *might* be adapted); (c) the open/closed companions `polarized-k3-remainder` share f,g.
8. **Non-polynomial denominators / higher q**: nothing; also the q=1 problem has not been linked to its q≥2 counterparts (which share the support).
9. **Dense random denominators**: no random or structured sweep at any degree (DiamondJM's ~1300-support sweep listed in memory covered q=1 open lanes with "natural degree-6 supports"; for this instance the log shows only the 11 structural + 1 cone test).
10. **Nonexistence side**: nothing rigorous; the only exclusions are the modular colon-space dimensions (d ≤ 5) and 12 fixed denominators.
