### 1.2 Structural presentation (4+2 split, "suspension")

F = H(X0,X1,Y0,Y1) + B(X2,Y2) with

```
f(X0,X1) = X0^6 + (5/7) X0^5 X1 + (10/7) X0 X1^5 + X1^6            (binary sextic, X-block)
g(Y0,Y1) = Y0^6 + (5/7) Y0^5 Y1 + (15/7) Y0 Y1^5 + Y1^6            (binary sextic, Y-block)
K(X0,X1,Y0,Y1) = (5/7) X0^3 X1 Y0 Y1 + (10/7) X0 X1 Y0^3 Y1 = (5/7) X0 X1 Y0 Y1 (X0^2 + 2 Y0^2)   (coupling)
H = f + g + K                                                        (core, 4 variables)
B(X2,Y2) = X2^6 + (15/7) X2^4 Y2^2 + (10/7) X2^2 Y2^4 + Y2^6 = b(X2^2, Y2^2),  b(u,v) = u^3 + (15/7)u^2 v + (10/7) u v^2 + v^3
```

Observations:
* The coupling K is `X0X1Y0Y1 · (5/7)(X0² + 2Y0²)`; both coupling monomials have (X-degree, Y-degree) = (4,2). H has no (2,4) or (0,6)-mixed part. K is the same shape as the holdout chain link X0^3X1Y0Y1 (used in `holdout`, `one-reflection`, `reflection-coupled-fano` etc.), plus its (1,1,3,1) partner.
* f(X0,X1) is **exactly** the X-block that appears inside P(u,v,w) of `crossed-ternary-asymmetric` and `split-ternary-q2` (coefficients 1, 5/7, 10/7, 1). g(Y0,Y1) has the different pattern (1, 5/7, 15/7, 1). The pair (f, g) with these coefficients also appears in `polarized-k3-remainder`.
* B is the only separately even block: B is even in X2 and in Y2 separately and is a cubic in (X2², Y2²). The target has X2-degree 0 and Y2-degree 0.
* The core H is *not* further decomposable linearly: `hessianSymmetrizerDimension: 2` (identity and the 4+2 projector). In particular there is no 3+3 split and no 2+2+2 split.
* **"Suspension"/Thom–Sebastiani**: X = {H + B = 0} is the join (Thom–Sebastiani sum) of the core and the binary form. Its cohomology decomposes via the cyclic covers T_H: u^3 = H ⊂ P(1,1,1,1,2) (Calabi–Yau threefold: weighted adjunction gives K = O(6 − (1+1+1+1+2)) = O(0)) and C_B: w^3 = B ⊂ P(1,1,2) (genus 4). Precisely (see §1.6), X/⟨r2 r5⟩ is birational to (T_H × C_B)/μ3 and the τ-invariant primitive (3,1)-classes of the sector containing A sit in [H^3(T_H) ⊗ H^1(C_B)]^{μ3}.

### 1.3 Symmetry group [NEW, PROVEN by exhaustive search over (μ12)^6]

Projective diagonal symmetry group G ⊂ PGL6 of F: **order 24**, G ≅ Z/6 × Z/2 × Z/2, generated by

```
h  = diag(1, 1, ζ6, 1, 1, ζ6)        (scales the binary block; B is homogeneous of degree 6 in (X2,Y2))
r2 = diag(1, 1, −1, 1, 1, 1)         (reflection X2 → −X2)
s  = diag(1, 1, 1, −1, −1, 1)        (paired sign on (Y0,Y1); H has only even total (Y0,Y1)-degree)
```
with r5 := Y2 → −Y2 equal to h^3 r2, and the structural involution τ = diag(1,1,1,−1,−1,−1) = s · r5. The `checks.json` value `projectiveDiagonalGroupOrder: 24` is confirmed. No reflection in X0, X1, Y0, Y1 individually is a symmetry (X0^5X1 and Y0^5Y1 are odd in each of their variables), and the paired signs s0: (X0,Y0)→− and s1: (X1,Y1)→− are broken by X0^5X1 (degree 5 in (X0,Y0), degree 1 in (X1,Y1)). So the paired-sign group of §4.2 is not available; only s2 = r2 r5 = h^3 survives.

**Non-diagonal symmetries**: none recorded by anyone; none obvious. The coupling K and the asymmetric coefficient patterns of f and g (5/7,10/7 vs 5/7,15/7) rule out the exchanges X↔Y, X0↔X1, Y0↔Y1 and the block swap used in 3A.4. The (X2,Y2) block B = b(X2²,Y2²) has, besides r2, r5, h, the exchange X2 ↔ Y2 **only if** b is palindromic — it is not (15/7 ≠ 10/7). (A linear change of (X2,Y2) alone can diagonalize b into three distinct linear factors; see §5, E6.)

### 1.4 Residue character [NEW, PROVEN]

For a diagonal element with weights a_j (mod 12 here), a monomial numerator Z^e has residue character Σ(e_j+1)a_j (the +1's record the volume form, §3.3 of Chapter 3). For A = X0^3 Y0^3 (e = (3,0,0,3,0,0)):

| element | weights (mod 12, ζ12 units) | χ_A | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| h | (0,0,2,0,0,2) | 4 → ζ3 | the residue picks up ζ3 under ζ6-scaling of the binary block |
| h² | (0,0,4,0,0,4) | 8 → ζ3² | |
| h³ = r2 r5 | (0,0,6,0,0,6) | 0 → +1 | invariant under the simultaneous sign of (X2,Y2) |
| r2 | (0,0,6,0,0,0) | 6 → −1 | anti-invariant under X2 → −X2 |
| r5 | (0,0,0,0,0,6) | 6 → −1 | anti-invariant under Y2 → −Y2 (benediamond's observation) |
| s | (0,0,0,6,6,0) | 6 → −1 | anti-invariant under (Y0,Y1) → −(Y0,Y1) |
| τ = s r5 | (0,0,0,6,6,6) | 0 → +1 | the required global invariance |
| r2 s | (0,0,6,6,6,0) | 0 → +1 | |

Stabilizer of χ_A: the Klein four-group K0 = {1, τ, r2r5, r2s}. The χ_A-isotypic component of H^4_prim(X) (computed in the Fermat ring C[Z]/(Z_i^5), valid by constancy of the equivariant Koszul Hilbert series on the smooth locus, as in §4.2) has Hodge dimensions **(32, 80, 32)**, rank 144. The A-residue is one of the 32 (3,1)-classes of this sector (its nonvanishing in the Jacobian ring: `targetNonzeroInReducedJacobianRing: true`; mod 5 the partials reduce to Z_i^5 and X0^3Y0^3 has exponents < 5).

Meaning of the characters: χ(h) = ζ3 says the class is in the sector where the binary cover C_B contributes a nontrivial deck character; χ(r5) = χ(r2) = −1 says the binary factor is anti-invariant under both reflections of B (so it lies in the σ-anti-invariant Prym part of C_B, not in the elliptic quotient C_B/σ); χ(s) = −1 says the core factor is anti-invariant under the (Y0,Y1) paired sign.

### 1.5 Ages (Kollár–Larsen test) [NEW]

Minimal age over fixed points on P^5 (then restricted to X) for each nontrivial element, computed from the weight lists: h: 1/3 (fixed surface {X2=Y2=0} ∩ X = S_H:={H=0}⊂P^3; normal eigenvalues ζ6, ζ6; both normal directions lie in T_X since dF has no X2,Y2 components there), h²: 2/3, h³=r2r5: 1, r2: 1/2, r5: 1/2, s: 1, τ: 3/2, r2 s: 3/2, h r2 etc.: ≥ 5/6. Consequences:
* X/⟨h⟩ and X/⟨h²⟩ are uniruled (Kollár–Larsen, §3.3 "Finite quotients beyond reflections"), so the h-invariant (equivalently h²-invariant) middle cohomology is divisor-supported. **But χ_A(h²) = ζ3² ≠ 1**: the target is in the complementary ζ3-character. This is the precise sense in which the instance is a "residual" lane: the natural cyclic quotient serves the other sector.
* X/r2 and X/r5 are the Fano reflection quotients (age 1/2), serving targets odd in X2 resp. Y2 — not A.
* No element of K0 = Stab(χ_A) has age < 1 (`juniorElementsFixingTarget: []` confirmed). So no diagonal cyclic quotient argument can serve A.

### 1.6 Geometry of the join and location of the target [GEOM; characters PROVEN]

Put u³ = H, w³ = B. On X, (X2,Y2) = λ·(x2,y2) with λ² = −u/w, so X/⟨h³⟩ (h³ = (X2,Y2)→−(X2,Y2) is λ→−λ) is birational to (T_H × C_B)/μ3 with μ3 acting by (u,w) → (ζu, ζw). Hence
H^4(X)^{h³} ⊇ [H^3(T_H) ⊗ H^1(C_B)]^{opp} ⊕ (algebraic/Tate terms), "opp" = pairs of opposite deck characters.

* **T_H** (CY threefold, triple cover of P^3 branched along the smooth sextic surface S_H = {H=0}): Jacobian ring C[X0,X1,Y0,Y1]/(∂H) ⊗ C[u]/(u²), Hilbert series of R_H = (1+t+t²+t³+t⁴)^4 = 1,4,10,20,35,52,68,80,85,80,68,52,35,20,10,4,1. Residue grading: H^{3−k,k} ↔ weighted degree 6k; u^a m with character ζ^{a+1}. Eigenspace Hodge numbers (h^{3,0},h^{2,1},h^{1,2},h^{0,3}): **ζ: (1, 68, 35, 0)**, **ζ²: (0, 35, 68, 1)**; b_3(T_H) = 208. T_H is not Fano, not uniruled (K=0).
* **C_B** (genus 4): H^{1,0}_{ζ²} = ⟨dt/w⟩ (dim 1), H^{1,0}_{ζ} = ⟨dt/w², t dt/w², t² dt/w²⟩ (dim 3). σ = r5 acts: σ-invariant holomorphic line t dt/w² (→ the elliptic quotient E: w³ = b(v), v=t², j(E)=0), σ-anti-invariant: dt/w, dt/w², t²dt/w² (→ the Prym P, dim 3, Q(ζ3)-signature (2,1)).
* The (3,1)-part of [H^3(T_H)⊗H^1(C_B)]^{opp}: (3,0)_ζ⊗(0,1)_{ζ²}: 1·3 = 3; (2,1)_ζ⊗(1,0)_{ζ²}: 68·1 = 68; (2,1)_{ζ²}⊗(1,0)_ζ: 35·3 = 105. The target A·U (benediamond: core numerator A·U has character ζ6² → ζ3 on T_H; binary numerator V³ → ζ3² on C_B) lies in **H^{2,1}(T_H)_ζ ⊗ H^{1,0}(C_B)_{ζ²} = H^{2,1}(T_H)_ζ ⊗ ⟨dt/w⟩**, a 68-dimensional (3,1)-space; intersecting with the τ-character (s-anti-invariant on the core side, which halves the core space roughly) is consistent with the 32-dimensional χ_A-sector found in §1.4 (the Fermat count gives 32 = dim of the s-anti-invariant, τ-invariant part of this tensor's (3,1) piece together with its χ constraints; exact equality not separately verified).

### 1.7 Smoothness [PROVEN]
`checks.json`: `smoothReductionPrime: 5`; mod 5 all coefficients 5/7, 10/7, 15/7 vanish and F ≡ Σ Z_i^6, whose partials are 6Z_i^5 — a regular sequence; hence the Jacobian ring has the Fermat Hilbert function and X is smooth over Q (smoothness is open and the reduction is smooth). Equivalently: F is smooth iff S_H = {H=0} ⊂ P^3 is a smooth surface and B is squarefree (singular points of the join come from singular points of the summands: ∇H = 0, ∇B = 0 simultaneously with H = −B); both hold mod 5.

### 1.8 Colon condition and the certificate system [PROVEN structure; dims SCREEN at p = 2^31−1]
Euler normalization (Chapter 1) makes N(F) = A·D exact, so a necessary condition is **A·D ∈ J(F)**, i.e. D ∈ (J(F):A). dims of {D Y-even of degree d : A·D ∈ J(F)} / J(F)_d: d=1..4: 0; d=5: 0 (kernel = J_5, dim 3); **d=6: 20** (kernel 38, J_6 has dim 18); **d=7: 63** (kernel 126, J_7 dim 63). d=0: A ∉ J(F).
