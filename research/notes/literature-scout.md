## 0. Bottom line (ranked by potential impact on the lanes)

1. **Nothing published since Diamond's paper enlarges the solvable locus.** Diamond arXiv:2603.29157 (v1 2026-03-31, v2 2026-08-10, "typo-fixes and minor improvements") has **0 citations** (Semantic Scholar, 2026-11-02; OpenAlex has no record). Its Theorem 3.16 covers exactly the sextics f(U)+f(V) with f of Waring rank 3, i.e. the Fermat sextic after a linear change of coordinates (proof: Construction 3.9 for Fermat, then pull back along the change of coordinates); the paper claims "at the very least 17 projective dimensions within the 235-dimensional space of invariant sextics" (8 of them Shioda-Katsura). The book's families (Ch. 4: 107-dim all-target legal family, 50-dim strict family) already exceed this. The paper's own list of what is open coincides with the obstacles in our lanes: non-strict solutions ("we haven't managed to find non-strict solutions yet", Remark 3.14), general F, and the failure of the Fermat combinatorics for q>2 (Remark 3.7) - relevant to cycle-q2. Nothing to import beyond the geometric reading of the partial-Euler denominators dF(nu_I) as ramification divisors of the join fibration P^5 --> P^{i-1} x P^{5-i} (Section 3, p. 17), which the book already uses as "subset-Euler" denominators.

2. **Kuga-Satake algebraicity for a degree-2 K3 with rho <= 4 and End_Hdg(T) = Q is still open** (lane polarized-k3-remainder). The 2024-2026 progress (Markman arXiv:2509.23403: Weil classes algebraic on *all* abelian fourfolds of Weil type; Floccari arXiv:2501.02315; Floccari-Fu arXiv:2504.13607; Varesco arXiv:2304.02519 and Michigan Math. J. 2024; Poon, Math. Nachr. 2026 for rho = 14) all live at Picard rank >= 14-16 or at Kum_n-type hyper-Kählers. The only algebraicity theorem applying to a Picard-rank-2-to-4 double plane remains the non-symplectic-order-3 case (Bolognesi-Laterveer arXiv:2310.11981), already used in book Ch. 3a; the 2026-11-01 probe excluded order-2/3 automorphisms for this f. **No literature shortcut; the lane needs its own geometry.** Saller's Oct-2026 Zenodo notes (RM-by-Q(sqrt5) family, HC for X={F(x)+F(y)=0}) are the closest published computations on the same fourfold type but concern (2,2) classes and RM K3s.

3. **Pryms with Z[zeta3]-action behave generically for the four-plus-two block.** Rains arXiv:2512.19597 (Dec 2025, geometric monodromy of cyclic Pryms is sandwiched between a unitary group and its derived group; j=0 elliptic surfaces = degree-6 cyclic covers), Zarhin arXiv:0809.4887 (absolutely simple Pryms of trigonal curves), Laga-Shnidman arXiv:2308.15297 (bielliptic Picard curves: Prym surfaces with QM by the discriminant-6 order), and Moonen's special families (Doc. Math. 2010; the genus-4 family y^3 = sextic is a Deligne-Mostow ball quotient, weights (1/3)^6) all say: the Prym of C_B -> E with Q(zeta3)-signature (2,1) is a point of a Picard modular surface, and only CM points have extra endomorphisms. Moonen's Table 1 (confirmed from the paper) contains the genus-4 family y^3 = sextic (m=3, N=6, a=(1,...,1)) as a special subvariety, so the four-plus-two Prym lives on a Picard modular surface whose non-CM points have End^0 = Q(zeta3). This **confirms the probe's exclusion** (End^0 = Q(zeta3), absolutely simple, non-CM) and shows no Prym-splitting route exists for the given B.

4. **Cyclic-cover eigen-sectors with singular branch loci now have an exact Hodge-filtration formula**: Hiatt arXiv:2608.25947 (Aug 2026, mixed Hodge modules): pi_* of the cover's Hodge module decomposes into eigen-pieces with Gr^F given by R phi_*(Omega^p(log E)(D_i^{>0})) (x) L^{-i} (Esnault-Viehweg sheaves), and Y is Du Bois iff lct(D) >= i/N for all i (rational iff >). **Usable now** for the residual lanes (cyclic-triple-residual, four-plus-two-residual, symmetric-sextic-residual, klein-coupled-character) to decide, without resolving, which holomorphic 2-forms survive in a given character on a singular cyclic-cover support, i.e. to prove "this support cannot carry the target's (3,1)-character" statements rigorously.

## A. The generalized Hodge conjecture for sextic fourfolds with involution (Voisin's question; Diamond)

**A1. B. E. Diamond, "Hodge Structures in Sextic Fourfolds Equipped with an Involution", arXiv:2603.29157, v1 2026-03-31, v2 2026-08-10.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29157 , HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29157v2 , local text `/tmp/hodge/ext/litscout-dl/diamond-v2.txt`.
- Setting: F = f(X0,X1,X2) - f(Y0,Y1,Y2) (Shioda construction; cites Shioda-Katsura [SK79, Rem. 1.10]); the iota-invariant H^4(X,Q)^+ has Hodge coniveau 1; GHC predicts a divisor Y with H^+ in ker(H^4(X) -> H^4(X-Y)).
- **Theorem 3.16 (verbatim modulo spacing):** "Let f(X0,X1,X2) be a ternary sextic of Waring rank 3 whose associated plane curve C is smooth. Then the smooth sextic X defined by f(X)-f(Y) is such that the iota-invariant coniveau-1 Hodge substructure H^4(X,Q)^+ is annihilated outside of a divisor Y." Proof: Construction 3.9 (Fermat) + pullback along the linear change L_0,L_1,L_2 (independent because C is smooth). Waring rank 3 = 8-dimensional projective family of smooth plane sextics (of 27); "at the very least 17 projective dimensions within the 235-dimensional space of all invariant sextics; that they might be pushed further seems plausible. (Only 8 of our 17 dimensions fall under the Shioda-Katsura construction.)"
- Algebraic reformulation (Question 3.1 = the hodge.computer contract): for q in {1,2} and each sigma-anti-invariant A in R_{6q}, a vector field v with homogeneous rational components of degree 6q-5, denominators not divisible by F, with F^{q+1} | A - q dF(v) + F div(v). Theorem 2.7 (vanishing criterion with pole order <= q), Theorem 2.13 (algebraic primitive via Grothendieck comparison, *requires* a finite pole-order bound along X; Section 2.3 says producing an algebraic primitive without it "proves apparently essential" and they do not know how), Remark 2.6 (only a weak form of Deligne degeneration follows).
- Construction 3.2 / Lemma 3.5: denominators q nu_I(F), nu_I = sum_{i in I} Z_i d/dZ_i (partial Euler fields), nu_I(F) not in (F) because Lin(X) is finite (Poonen); geometrically V(dF(nu_I)) meets X in the ramification divisor of the join fibration P^5 --> P^{i-1} x P^{5-i}. Construction 3.9 (recursive Griffiths-Dwork-type reduction, extends Bostan-Lairez-Salvy 2013); Theorem 3.10; Lemma 3.8 (T^{(q)}(A)=0 for anti-invariant A).
- **Remark 3.7:** the Jacobian-reduction hypothesis (exponents <= 4) is necessary; for the degree-8 Fermat sixfold the subset condition fails at q=2 and Lemma 3.4 at q=3, so "the combinatorial fact fails as soon as q > 2". **Remark 3.12:** Lemma 3.8 is independent of q and Theorem 3.10 works for q in {0..4}; extension to general sigma-invariant sextics needs a replacement for Construction 3.2 (which needs the Fermat form). **Remark 3.14:** all solutions found are strict; "We don't expect that strict solutions should exist for general sigma-invariant sextics F; the fact that we haven't managed to find non-strict solutions yet suggests that we're leaving something on the table."
- Explicitly open (also collected at https://www.emergentmind.com/open-problems/algebraic-primitive-without-finite-pole-order-hypothesis and https://www.emergentmind.com/open-problems/equivalence-parameterized-by-1-cycles-vs-geometric-coniveau-for-all-smooth-sextics ): Voisin's question on a nonempty Zariski-open set ("doing as much for general F seems difficult"); "parameterized by 1-cycles" <=> geometric coniveau 1 for all smooth sextics (follows from the Lefschetz standard conjecture; Voisin: true for general sextics); algebraic primitive without finite pole order; maximal Waring rank of ternary sextics (>= 12, De Paris 2016).
- Bibliography (all pre-2015 except [ABC20] Andre-Baldassarri-Cailotto 2020, [De 16] De Paris 2016, [GW23] Gortz-Wedhorn 2023, [Voi15]). Acknowledges Math, Inc. No software, no Lean, no website in the paper.
- Citations: 0 (Semantic Scholar 2026-11-02). The author's site https://benediamond.github.io/ (updated 2026-10-20) lists the preprint as "submitted", lists him as Staff Mathematician at Math, Inc. (2026) and as creator of hodge.computer; no blog posts.

**A2. C. Voisin, "The generalized Hodge and Bloch conjectures are equivalent for general complete intersections, II", J. Math. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 22 (2015) 491-517, arXiv:1403.3904.** https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.3904 - the reference behind the hodge.computer overview: unconditional weak form of the GHC<=>Bloch equivalence (if cohomology of a general very ample hypersurface is parameterized by c-cycles, then CH_i = 0 for i <= c-1); the involution/K3-double-plane remark is inside the paper, not in the abstract.

**A3. C. Voisin, "Hodge and generalized Hodge conjectures, coniveau and algebraic cycles", J. Open Math. Problems 1 (2025) 16-51** (received Jan 2025). https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~claire.voisin/Articlesweb/JOMP_V01N01P02Paper.pdf , local text `/tmp/hodge/ext/litscout-dl/voisin-jomp-2025.txt`. Survey: Conjecture 4.6 (GHC), Conjecture 4.7 + Prop. 4.8 (Hodge coniveau c => Hodge structure comes from a variety of dimension k-2c; with HC implies GHC), Conjecture 5.12 (generalized Bloch), Conjectures 6.2/6.3 for complete intersections (decomposition of the diagonal / CH_i injective for i <= c-1 when n >= sum d_i + (c-1) d_r), Theorem 6.4 (Esnault-Levine-Viehweg / Otwinowska range), Theorem 6.5 = [Voi15] (for general hypersurfaces 6.2 and 6.3 are equivalent), and the remark that hypersurfaces of block-sum form f_1(block_1)+...+f_c(block_c) satisfy both conjectures [Voi96] (Thom-Sebastiani / Shioda-Katsura). The sextic-with-involution problem is not mentioned. Takeaway: as of 2025 coniveau-2 cases are known only in a handful of situations; nothing new for coniveau-1 of sextic fourfolds.

## C. Pryms and cyclic covers of curves (four-plus-two-residual, cyclic-triple-residual, symmetric-sextic-residual)

- **C1. E. M. Rains, "The monodromy of cyclic Pryms", arXiv:2512.19597 (Dec 2025, 49 pp.)** https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19597 : geometric monodromy on Tate modules of Pryms of cyclic covers in natural families (varying a tame ramification point) is sandwiched between a unitary group and its derived subgroup (few exceptions); applications to Selmer groups of j=0 (degree-6 cyclic) and j=1728 elliptic surfaces. Implication: a general member of a family of Z[zeta3]-Pryms has End^0 = Q(zeta3); extra endomorphisms occur only on proper special loci.
- **C2. B. Moonen, "Special subvarieties arising from families of cyclic covers of the projective line", Doc. Math. 15 (2010) 793-819, arXiv:1006.3400** (pre-2015, listed for the classification; local text `/tmp/hodge/ext/litscout-dl/moonen-1006.3400.txt`): Theorem 3.6: Z(m,N,a) is special iff (m,N,a) is one of the twenty triples of Table 1. **Confirmed from Table 1:** the row (genus 4, m = 3, N = 6, a = (1,1,1,1,1,1)) is present (references: Shimura's example S(2), Mostow M23, de Jong-Noot (1.3.1)), i.e. the genus-4 family y^3 = (binary sextic) - exactly the four-plus-two block curve C_B: w^3 = B(X2,Y2) - is a 3-dimensional special subvariety (ball quotient, Deligne-Mostow weights (1/3)^6) of A_4 with Z[zeta3]-action. Other table rows with m = 6: (genus 2, m=6, N=4, a=(2,3,3,4)), (genus 3, m=6, N=4, (1,3,4,4)), (genus 4, m=6, N=4, (1,1,1,3)), (genus 4, m=6, N=4, (1,1,2,2)), (genus 4, m=6, N=5, (2,2,2,3,3)), (genus 6, m=10,...), none of which is the six-branch-point degree-6 cover u^6 = sextic (m=6, N=6, (1,...,1) is explicitly *not* special, Moonen 7.2.4). Consequence for four-plus-two-residual: the Prym of C_B -> E (Q(zeta3)-signature (2,1)) varies in a 2-dimensional special subfamily (a Picard modular surface); its generic member is absolutely simple with End^0 = Q(zeta3), extra endomorphisms occur exactly at the countably many CM points, and the probe (point counts, non-CM) shows the instance's B is not one of them.
- **C3. Y. Zarhin, "Absolutely simple Prymians of trigonal curves", arXiv:0809.4887** (2008/9): Galois-theoretic construction of absolutely simple principally polarized Pryms not isomorphic to Jacobians. **C4. J. Laga, A. Shnidman, "The geometry and arithmetic of bielliptic Picard curves", arXiv:2308.15297 (v3 Dec 2024)**: for y^3 = x^4 + a x^2 + b the Prym surface has QM by the discriminant-6 order, Torelli theorem, rational torsion classified - the genus-3 analogue of the four-plus-two Prym (there the quotient is elliptic with j=0 and the Prym is 3-dimensional, so this result does not transfer, but the method - explicit QM from the two commuting automorphisms - is the pattern to check if a lane ever has a bielliptic cyclic cover). **C5. T. Alexandrou, arXiv:2110.02093 (2021)**: generic isogeny decomposition of Pryms of cyclic branched covers (eigen-decomposition). **C6. A. Shatsila, arXiv:2609.14135 (Sept 2026)**: Prym-Torelli for etale cyclic covers of odd prime degree of hyperelliptic curves (d=3 via trigonal pencils). **C7. Borowka-Naranjo-Ortega-Shatsila, arXiv:2510.01330 (2025)**: Prym map of etale cyclic covers of hyperelliptic curves injective for d >= 6 not an odd prime power; Naranjo-Ortega-Spelta, Forum Math. Sigma 2024: P_d generically injective for primes d >= 11 with (d-1)/2 prime.
- Lane four-plus-two-residual: the probe (2026-11-01) found the Prym P of T_B -> E' absolutely simple with End^0 = Q(zeta3) and non-CM (point counts p <= 400). C1/C2 say this is the generic situation; an elliptic contraction would require B to be a CM point. The instance's B is fixed, so the route is closed; literature adds no alternative correspondence.

## F. Cyclic covers, eigen-sectors, singular supports

- **F1. S. Hiatt, "Cyclic covers via mixed Hodge modules", arXiv:2608.25947 (26 Aug 2026).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25947 . For any variety X, line bundle L, s in H^0(L^N) with Cartier divisor D, pi: Y -> X the N-fold cover: pi_* Q^H_Y = Q^H_X (+) j_! V, V = (+)_{i=1}^{N-1} H_i (Thm 0.1/2.4); for a log resolution phi: Xtilde -> X, E = phi^*D_red, D_i^{>0} / D_i^{>=0} the floor-type divisors: pi_* Omega-underline^p_Y = Omega^p_X (+) (+)_i R phi_*(Omega^p_{Xtilde}(log E)(D_i^{>0})) (x) L^{-i} and the dual with D_i^{>=0} (Thm 0.2; Esnault-Viehweg sheaves as Gr^F of the eigen-pieces); singular X via cubical hyperresolutions (Thm 0.4; Kovacs' simplification for general D); Y Du Bois iff lct(D) >= i/N for all i, rational iff lct(D) > i/N (Thm 0.3, Cor. 3.12), rational homology manifold if D_i^{>0} = D_i^{>=0} for all i (gcd(N,a_j)=1 in snc case); vanishing of R^q phi_* for p+q > n (Cor. 3.10), and H^i(X, Omega-underline^0 (x) L^{-1}) = 0 for i < n - lcdef(X) (Thm 0.5). No Hodge ideals, no coniveau. **Use:** exact computation of h^{2,0} (and the character decomposition) of a singular cyclic-cover support without resolving it, i.e. rigorous "which (2,0)-forms, in which character, survive on this support" statements for the residual lanes.

## G. Joins, Thom-Sebastiani sums, planes, splittings

- **G1. J. Duque Franco, R. Villaflor Loyola, "Periods of join algebraic cycles", arXiv:2312.17222 (v4 Sept 2025; Ann. Sc. Norm. Super. Pisa, to appear)** https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17222 : general formula for the cycle class of the join J(Z_1,Z_2) of two cycles inside smooth hypersurfaces f(x)+g(y) = 0 in terms of their periods; a cycle is a join iff its Artin-Gorenstein algebra is the tensor product of those of the factors; infinitely many Hodge loci with linear-cycle-type tangent codimension from joins of 0-cycles. **Use:** for four-plus-two-residual (F = H(X0,X1,Y0,Y1)+B(X2,Y2)), short-power-presentation and the split-type lanes, the natural divisors (cones over {H=0} from the six roots of B = joins; joins of curves with points) have computable Gysin images and characters; gives an a-priori, citable statement of which character sectors join-supports reach (reproduces the complementary-character obstruction).
- **G2. R. Kloosterman, "On a conjecture on Hodge loci of linear combinations of linear subvarieties", arXiv:2312.12363 (v2 July 2025)**: counterexamples to Movasati's conjecture for cubics/quartics with two k-planes (k >= 5); nothing on sextic fourfolds or planes (k=2). For eight-plane-configuration no new literature.
- **G3.** Voisin 2025 survey: see A3 ([Voi96] block-sum hypersurfaces satisfy the Bloch-type and decomposition conjectures; Schoen 1988 "Hodge classes on self-products of a variety with an automorphism" cited).
- **G4.** Shioda-Katsura inductive structure (Tohoku 1979) remains the only "pullback/decomposition" reference used by everyone (Diamond, Saller, book Ch. 3a); no 2015-2026 generalization to F = G(Q) with quadrics or to S_3 x S_3 pullbacks was found (searches: "hypersurface polynomial in quadratic forms Hodge structure", "symmetric hypersurface quotient weighted projective space elementary symmetric"; only Batyrev-Cox alg-geom/9306011, Garcia-Nemethi alg-geom/9701003, Kelly "Quotients of hypersurfaces in weighted projective space" arXiv:0905.2099, Raukh arXiv:2506.04094 (integral cohomology of weighted hypersurfaces), and the physics paper arXiv:2606.05530 (invariant Hodge classes of weighted Fermat hypersurfaces under permutation symmetries)).
- **G5. J.-M. Hwang, "Symmetrizer group of a projective hypersurface", arXiv:2501.08633 (Jan 2025; J. Math. Soc. Japan, to appear)** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08633 : for a non-cone hypersurface, an abelian linear algebraic group attached to the symmetric form describing all polynomials with the same Jacobian ideal (explains Ueda-Yoshinaga and Wang); its diagonalizable part detects the Sebastiani-Thom (sum of forms in disjoint variables) property, its unipotent part relates to singularities. **Use:** a published, rigorous criterion for "F admits no hidden Thom-Sebastiani splitting" (the four-plus-two dossier's 2-dimensional Hessian-symmetrizer computation; short-power-presentation's 8-power presentation; rank-one-biquartic; symmetric-sextic-residual).
